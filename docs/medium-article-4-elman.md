# The Elman Induction: Hypnotize Someone in 4 Minutes (Step-by-Step Guide)

**The first time I saw the Elman induction work, I didn't believe it.**

A student volunteer sat in a chair. The instructor asked her to close her eyes and relax her eyelids. Thirty seconds later, he told her to try opening them. She couldn't. Her eyes were locked shut — and she hadn't been "hypnotized" yet. She had just followed a simple instruction to relax.

Four minutes later, she was in somnambulism — the deepest state of hypnosis — responding to suggestions as if they were reality.

I had seen stage hypnosis before. I assumed it was fake, or at least exaggerated. But this wasn't a stage. It was a training room with 15 students, and I was next in line to try it.

That was my introduction to the Dave Elman induction — and it changed how I think about hypnosis entirely.

## What Makes the Elman Induction Different

Most people think hypnosis requires a swinging pocket watch, a monotone voice, and 20 minutes of "you are getting sleepy." The Elman induction throws all of that out.

Dave Elman was a stage hypnotist in the 1940s who later taught doctors and dentists how to use hypnosis in clinical settings. His method is designed for speed and reliability. The goal: produce somnambulism in 3-5 minutes, every time.

The genius of the Elman induction is that it doesn't ask the subject to "try to relax." It gives them specific, testable tasks. Each task proves to the subject that hypnosis is working — which makes the next step easier.

---

## The 4 Steps

### Step 1: Eye Catalepsy (The Critical Factor Bypass)

This is the foundation. If this step doesn't work, you don't proceed.

Ask the subject to close their eyes and focus all their attention on their eyelids. Tell them to relax the eyelids completely — so relaxed that the muscles stop working. Then ask them to try to open their eyes.

If the eyes stay closed, the subject has just experienced their first hypnotic phenomenon. Their conscious mind now has proof that "something is happening." This bypasses the critical factor — the mental filter that normally rejects suggestions.

If the eyes open, you simply say "that's fine, close them again and go deeper" and repeat. Most people lock on the first or second attempt.

> *"Close your eyes. Focus all your attention on your eyelids. Relax them completely. Let them feel so heavy, so relaxed, that they stop working. In a moment, try to open them. If they don't open, they're locked. Try now. They don't work. Good. That's proof your body is already responding."*

### Step 2: Hand Drop Test (Body Relaxation Check)

Lift the subject's hand gently. Tell them not to help you — let it be dead weight. Drop it.

If the hand falls like a wet rag, the body is relaxed. If it stays rigid or the subject "helps" you lift it, they're still in conscious control. Repeat the eye catalepsy and try again.

This is where beginners often fail. The subject wants to cooperate — they'll unconsciously assist you in lifting their hand. You must explicitly say: "Don't help me. Let me do the lifting."

> *"I'm going to lift your hand. Don't help me — let it be completely limp. When I drop it, you'll go twice as deep. [Lift and drop.] Deeper. Twice as deep."*

### Step 3: Reverse Counting (Mind Relaxation)

Now the body is relaxed, but the mind is still active. The solution: give the mind a task that becomes progressively harder.

Ask the subject to count backward from 100 out loud. With each number, they become "twice as relaxed." The counting occupies the conscious mind while the subconscious opens.

The key insight: it doesn't matter if they count correctly. In fact, losing count is a sign it's working. When the voice slows, then stops — the mind has let go.

> *"Count backward from 100. With each number, your mind becomes twice as relaxed. 100 — more relaxed. 99 — more relaxed. If you lose count, just make up numbers. The counting isn't important — the relaxation is."*

### Step 4: Fractionation (Deepening)

This is the accelerator. Ask the subject to open their eyes, then close them and go "twice as deep." Repeat 3-4 times. Each cycle deepens the trance exponentially.

Fractionation works because each time the subject opens their eyes, they briefly return to full consciousness — and then drop back into trance even faster. The contrast between "awake" and "trance" makes the trance feel deeper each time.

> *"Open your eyes. Close them — twice as deep. Open — close — deeper. Open — close — deepest. Now stay there."*

## Why It Works (The Science)

The Elman induction isn't magic. It's a structured protocol that leverages three psychological principles:

1. **Response set** — each successful step makes the next step more likely. The subject thinks "I couldn't open my eyes, so maybe I can't lift my hand either."

2. **Fractionation** — rapid cycling between trance and wakefulness deepens each subsequent trance. This is well-documented in hypnosis research.

3. **Task absorption** — the counting step occupies conscious attention, preventing the subject from analyzing or resisting. It's the same principle as mindfulness meditation.

A 2016 Stanford fMRI study confirmed that hypnosis produces measurable changes in brain activity — it's not placebo, and it's not "just relaxation." The Elman induction is one of the most reliable ways to produce these changes.

## Common Mistakes Beginners Make

**Going too fast.** The Elman induction should take 4-5 minutes. If you rush, the subject can't follow. Time yourself.

**Skipping the hand drop test.** If the body isn't relaxed, the mind won't follow. Don't skip this step just because the eyes locked.

**Not repeating when it fails.** If the eyes open, repeat. If the hand stays rigid, repeat. Persistence is part of the protocol — it's not a failure, it's feedback.

**Improvising before mastering the basics.** The Elman sequence is precise for a reason. Practice it until it's automatic before you modify it.

## Three Variations for Different Subjects

### For Anxious Subjects
Skip the hand drop test. Go straight from eye catalepsy to counting. Anxious subjects may tense up when touched — don't force it.

### For Already-Relaxed Subjects
If the pre-talk has already put the subject in a light trance, use fractionation alone. Three open-close cycles can be enough.

### Full Script (Practice This)
The complete Elman induction from start to finish. Practice until it becomes automatic — you should be able to deliver it while thinking about what therapeutic work comes next.

## What I Learned From Practicing This

The Elman induction taught me something counterintuitive: **hypnosis isn't about what you do to someone. It's about what you help them do themselves.**

The eye catalepsy works because the subject relaxes their own eyelids. The hand drops because they stop holding it up. The counting stops because their own mind lets go.

Your job as the hypnotist is to give clear instructions and get out of the way. The subject does the actual work.

This is why the Elman induction is so reliable — it doesn't depend on your "charisma" or "hypnotic voice." It depends on a sequence of testable steps that anyone can follow.

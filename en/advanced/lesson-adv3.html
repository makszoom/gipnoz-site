About This Lesson

Not all therapy happens in a structured format. Some of Kein's most powerful sessions began spontaneously — triggered by a word, an object, or an unexpected emotional reaction. This lesson teaches how to recognize these moments and use them for regression therapy.

Kein shares two powerful cases: Bill, the "tough guy" with a military bearing who collapsed into tears when confronted with his hidden fear, and the deathbed therapy protocol for unresolved grief. The common thread: strong emotions (anger, tears) during a session are signals, not problems.

Bill — The Tough Guy

Bill walked into Kein's office looking like a Marine recruit: crew cut, combat boots, camouflage pants. His tattoos read "Death is my profession." He spent the first part of the session telling Kein how tough he was, how he knew 27 ways to kill a man with his bare hands. Kein listened, then asked: "Bill, can I ask you a question? What are you so afraid of?"

Bill went ballistic. He jumped up, knocked his chair over, backed across the room screaming that he wasn't afraid of anyone. He kept backing up until he tripped over a beanbag chair and fell — and then he started sobbing. Kein didn't hesitate. He ran to Bill, slapped his forehead, shouted "Sleep!" and took him straight into regression. "If you're given a gift like that," Kein says, "don't waste it. The opportunity may not come again."

Deathbed Therapy

When a client has lost someone close, there is almost always unfinished business — words unsaid, hidden anger at the deceased for leaving, guilt about things done or not done. Kein's deathbed therapy protocol guides the subject through expressing everything they never got to say: anger, love, forgiveness, goodbye. The release is profound and often life-changing.

Recognizing Emotional Signals

Kein teaches: if your client becomes angry during a specific part of the conversation, make a note of what you were discussing. If they start crying, make a note. These are not interruptions to the session — they are the session. The emotion is the door to the root cause. Walk through it.

🧠 Key Takeaway: Emotion is not the enemy of therapy — it's the pathway. When a client gets angry or starts crying during a session, don't calm them down. Follow the emotion. It's pointing directly at the root cause.

📝 Practice: Recognizing Signals

During your next practice session, pay close attention to emotional shifts in your subject. Note what topic preceded any change in emotion. Practice the "Sleep!" command as a rapid induction trigger.

Emotional Release Prompt

"It's okay to feel this. Don't hold it back. Let it come. This feeling has been inside you for a long time — it's time to let it out. I'm right here. You're safe. Let it go. When you're ready, we'll find where this feeling started — and we'll release it at the source."

About This Lesson

This lesson covers two major topics: the business side of hypnosis and the Corridor Technique for age regression. Building a practice requires the same dedication as learning hypnosis — and this lesson provides specific, actionable strategies for both.

The second half introduces the Corridor Technique for age regression — a powerful visualization method where the subject walks down a corridor of doors, each representing a year of their life. When they stop at a door, they enter the room and re-experience the significant events stored there.

Part 1: Office Setup & Marketing

Dedicated office space. Even if you work from home, your office must feel professional — not like a living room. A separate room, a proper desk, a therapy chair, and professional decor signal to the client's subconscious that this is a place of healing.

Professional brochures. Print tri-fold brochures that state who you are and what you treat. Attach three business cards to each brochure. Include a "10% off first session" coupon so you can track which distribution methods produce results.

The 5-Mile Walk. Walk 5 miles daily in your target area, visiting every business on both sides of the street. Enter each establishment, approach one person, and hand them the brochure with three sentences: "Hi, I just opened my practice. Here's my brochure. Have a great day." Then leave. Do not hard-sell. The psychology: people who don't need hypnosis will pass the brochure to someone who does.

Doctor referrals. Follow up with local doctors using professional letters. Introduce yourself, describe your specialty, and offer to work with their patients. Medical referrals build credibility and provide a steady client stream.

Part 2: The Corridor Technique for Age Regression

Step 1: Achieve deep somnambulism. The subject must be in a deep enough state to visualize vividly and access stored memories.

Step 2: "Imagine a long corridor. On each side are doors representing years of your life. The doors get younger as you walk." The subject begins walking slowly.

Step 3: "When you feel a pull toward any door — a curiosity, a strange feeling — raise your finger. We'll enter that room together." Some subjects feel a pull immediately; others need to walk slowly. Both are normal.

Step 4: Inside the room is a pedestal with two buttons — green and red. Pressing green projects a significant memory from that year onto a screen. The subject describes what they see and feel as an observer.

Step 5: Guide through the memory, process the emotion, then press the red button to release it. "Watch it fade. It no longer controls you." Continue until the root memory is identified and processed.

Handling Memory Recall

Some subjects will feel a pull immediately. Others need to walk slowly — encourage patience. Some will resist a particular door. That resistance itself is information. If they stop at a door and don't want to enter, ask: "What do you feel when you look at this door?" The answer reveals the issue without forcing entry.

🧠 Key Takeaway: Marketing and therapy are two halves of the same practice. You need clients (marketing) and skills to help them (age regression). The 5-mile walk method works because it's personal and consistent. The corridor technique works because it gives the subconscious a structured way to reveal what needs healing.

📝 Practice: The Corridor & Marketing

Practice the three scripts below. For the marketing variant, rehearse the 3-sentence delivery until it feels natural.

Variant 1: Corridor Induction

"You're standing at the entrance of a long corridor. Doors on both sides. Each door is a year of your life. Walk slowly. When you feel a pull — stop and raise your finger. We'll enter together."

Variant 2: Memory Processing

"You're in the room. There's a screen playing the memory. You're an observer. What do you see? What do you feel? When you're ready, press the red button. Watch it fade. It no longer controls you."

Variant 3: 5-Mile Walk Script

"Walk one side of the street. Enter every business. Hand out one brochure. Three sentences: 'Hi, I just opened my practice. Here's my brochure. Have a great day.' Repeat on the other side. Do this daily."

Adv4 — Marketing Your Practice & Age Regression

~120 minutes

🎬 Video will be added after upload to YouTube

About This Lesson

This lesson covers two major topics: the business side of hypnosis and the Corridor Technique for age regression. Building a practice requires the same dedication as learning hypnosis — and this lesson provides specific, actionable strategies for both.

The second half introduces the Corridor Technique for age regression — a powerful visualization method where the subject walks down a corridor of doors, each representing a year of their life. When they stop at a door, they enter the room and re-experience the significant events stored there.

Part 1: Office Setup & Marketing

Dedicated office space. Even if you work from home, your office must feel professional — not like a living room. A separate room, a proper desk, a therapy chair, and professional decor signal to the client's subconscious that this is a place of healing.

Professional brochures. Print tri-fold brochures that state who you are and what you treat. Attach three business cards to each brochure. Include a "10% off first session" coupon so you can track which distribution methods produce results.

The 5-Mile Walk. Walk 5 miles daily in your target area, visiting every business on both sides of the street. Enter each establishment, approach one person, and hand them the brochure with three sentences. Do not hard-sell. The psychology: people who don't need hypnosis will pass the brochure to someone who does.

Doctor referrals. Follow up with local doctors using professional letters. Introduce yourself, describe your specialty, and offer to work with their patients. Medical referrals build credibility and provide a steady client stream.

Part 2: The Corridor Technique for Age Regression

Step 1: Achieve deep somnambulism. The subject must be in a deep enough state to visualize vividly and access stored memories.

Step 2: Guide the subject into the corridor visualization using the script below.

Step 3: When the subject feels a pull toward a door, they raise a finger. Enter that room together.

Step 4: Inside the room, the subject presses a green button to project the memory onto a screen, then describes what they see as an observer.

Step 5: Guide through the memory, process the emotion, then press the red button to release it. Continue until the root memory is identified and processed.

"You are standing at the entrance of a long corridor. As you look down it, you can see doors on both sides — left and right — stretching far into the distance. Each door represents a year of your life. The ones closest to you are recent years. As you walk further down, the doors become younger, taking you back through your past. Begin walking slowly now. Feel your feet on the floor. Notice the lighting — soft and gentle. As you walk, pay attention to any door that draws your attention — a strange feeling, a pull, a curiosity. When you feel that pull, stop. Raise your finger to let me know. We will enter that room together. There is no rush. Take your time."

Handling Memory Recall

Some subjects will feel a pull immediately. Others need to walk slowly — encourage patience. Some will resist a particular door. That resistance itself is information. If they stop at a door and don't want to enter, ask: "What do you feel when you look at this door?" The answer reveals the issue without forcing entry.

Memory Processing Script

Once the subject has chosen a door and entered the room, guide the memory processing:

"You are now inside the room. In the center of the room there is a pedestal, and on it are two buttons — one green, one red. Press the green button now. As you do, a screen lights up on the wall. On that screen, a significant memory from this year begins to play. You are an observer — safe, detached, watching. Describe what you see. What is happening? Who is there? What do you feel as you watch? Take your time. There is no need to rush. When you have seen enough, when you have felt the emotion and understood it, press the red button. Watch the image on the screen begin to fade. Watch it dissolve, becoming dimmer and dimmer until it is gone. The memory remains, but it no longer controls you. The emotion has been released. You are free."

🧠 Key Takeaway: Marketing and therapy are two halves of the same practice. You need clients (marketing) and skills to help them (age regression). The 5-mile walk method works because it's personal and consistent. The corridor technique works because it gives the subconscious a structured way to reveal what needs healing.

Variant 3: 5-Mile Walk Script (Full Template)

"Today you will walk five miles. Choose your target area — a neighborhood, a main street, a business district. Walk one side of the street first. Enter every single business establishment you encounter. Approach one person inside — the receptionist, the manager, the person at the front desk. Hand them your brochure. Say exactly these three sentences: 'Hi, I just opened my practice. Here's my brochure. Have a great day.' That is all. Do not explain. Do not hard-sell. Do not linger. Thank them and leave. Repeat this at every business on that side of the street. When you reach the end, cross over and walk back down the other side, repeating the same process. Do this every single day. Consistency is the key. Some people will keep the brochure. Some will throw it away. Some will pass it to someone who needs it. That is the psychology — people who do not need hypnosis will hand your brochure to someone who does. Trust the process. Walk five miles every day."

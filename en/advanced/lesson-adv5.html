About This Lesson

Forgiveness therapy is one of Kein's most powerful therapeutic tools. The premise: until a person forgives those who have harmed them, they remain emotionally controlled by them. The anger and resentment keep the wound open. Forgiveness is not about excusing the behavior — it's about freeing the self from the burden.

Kein demonstrates the technique through the case of George, an alcoholic whose life was shaped by abandonment, abuse, and betrayal. The lesson follows the full protocol: establishing readiness, the forgiveness decision, and the regression work that makes forgiveness complete.

The 95% Rule

Kein estimates that 95% of clients say "yes" when asked if they're ready to forgive — if the therapist has built the case properly. The key is framing: explain that forgiveness is not for the other person, it's for themselves. Explain that holding onto anger is like drinking poison and expecting the other person to die. Only after genuine readiness does Kein proceed with the therapy.

Case Study: George

George was an alcoholic whose mother abandoned him at an orphanage before he was two, promising "I'll come back for you" — a promise she never kept. He was the smallest boy there, constantly beaten and bullied. At Christmas, a local Baptist preacher "volunteered" to host him — and sexually abused him for 10 days. His father had abandoned him before birth. Kein's forgiveness therapy helped George release the rage and self-hatred that drove his drinking.

When People Can't Forgive

Kein acknowledges that some people cannot forgive — and that is their choice. At the same conference, a Holocaust survivor with a concentration camp number tattooed on his arm refused to forgive Hitler. Kein respected his decision. "We can't help 100% of people," he says. "We can help 100% of people if they do what we say — but not 100% of people will do what we say."

🧠 Key Takeaway: Forgiveness therapy is the most liberating work a hypnotist can do. The 95% rule tells you most people are ready — if you frame it correctly. Those who refuse have the right to their choice. But for those who accept, the transformation is profound.

📝 Practice: Forgiveness Protocol

Practice the forgiveness decision question: "From what you now know, are you ready and willing to forgive this person?" If yes, proceed. If no, reschedule.

Forgiveness Decision

"I want you to understand something important. Forgiveness is not saying what they did was okay. It's not making excuses. It's choosing to stop carrying the weight. As long as you hold onto this anger, they are still controlling you from wherever they are. Are you ready to take that control back? Are you ready to forgive — for yourself?"

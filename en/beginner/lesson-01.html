About This Lesson

Gerald Kein opens the course by sharing his personal journey into hypnosis — starting with the very first book he ordered from the back of a Popular Mechanics magazine in 1955. This isn't a dry lecture on theory. It's a storytelling session about where hypnosis literature came from, who wrote the important books, and which ones are actually worth reading today.

You'll learn about the eccentric characters who shaped modern hypnosis — from Orman McGill (the "Elder Statesman of American Hypnotism") to Dave Elman, whose book Hypnotherapy Kein considers the single most important text ever written on the subject.

The Early Days of Hypnosis Literature

In the 1950s, hypnosis books were rare. Most were only available to licensed professionals, and even then, the information was limited. Kein found his first book through a mail-order ad — a notorious little volume called How to Hypnotize Women (written anonymously, later revealed to be by Orman McGill).

Another key text was 25 Lessons in Hypnotism, which Kein also ordered through Popular Mechanics. Despite its sensational packaging, this book contained solid foundational knowledge that Kein still recommends to students today.

Dave Elman's Hypnotherapy — The Must-Read

If you could only buy one book on hypnosis, Kein says this is it. Hypnotherapy by Dave Elman is a compact, concentrated volume based on Elman's live classroom recordings. It shows how Elman worked with patients on real medical issues — phobias, dentistry, anesthesia, insomnia, depression — with exact word patterns he used. This book is the foundation of the Elman induction method that forms the core of this course.

Other Recommended Reading

Kein also recommends Answer to Cancer by Steve Parkhill, one of his former students. The book covers work with people diagnosed with terminal illnesses who recovered through hypnotic intervention. Parkhill is currently documenting his work with a hospital for formal research publication.

For those interested in past life regression — a topic the course will cover later — Kein suggests Many Lives, Many Masters by Dr. Brian Weiss, a Miami psychiatrist who accidentally discovered past life regression while working with a difficult case. Freud and Hypnosis by Dr. Milton Kline is another good historical reference for understanding how hypnosis shaped early psychoanalysis.

🧠 Key Takeaway: Hypnosis education has come a long way from the sensational mail-order books of the 1950s. The best resource remains Dave Elman's Hypnotherapy — short, practical, and packed with techniques that still work today. If you buy one book, make it that one.

📝 Your First Task

Before the next lesson, take a few minutes to find Dave Elman's Hypnotherapy online. You don't need to read it cover to cover — just get a feel for the style. Pay attention to how Elman structures his inductions and how direct his language is.

The book is widely available on Amazon and other booksellers. Used copies are perfectly fine.

About This Lesson

This is the course orientation. It covers the structure of the training, the recommended preparatory reading, and the approach to learning hypnosis that will be used throughout the program. No prior experience is needed — every technique is taught from the ground up, starting with the simplest foundations and building toward advanced clinical protocols.

Hypnosis is a skill, not a theory. This entire course is designed around the principle that you learn by doing — by practicing each technique with a partner until it becomes automatic, then moving on to the next. The material is presented in a specific sequence, and each lesson builds on the previous one.

Course Structure

The training is divided into two levels. The Beginner-Intermediate course consists of 12 lessons covering the foundations: induction techniques, deepening methods, trance phenomena, and basic therapeutic protocols. The Advanced course follows with 6 lessons covering rapid inductions, therapeutic regression, anesthesia protocols, and advanced therapy techniques. Each lesson builds on the previous one, so the sequence matters — do not skip ahead.

Recommended Reading

Dave Elman's Hypnotherapy is the single most important text in the field. It is a compact, concentrated volume based on Elman's live classroom recordings, showing exactly how he worked with patients on real medical issues — phobias, dentistry, anesthesia, insomnia, depression — with the exact word patterns he used. This book is the foundation of the Elman induction method that forms the core of this course. Also recommended are the works of Orman McGill on hypnotic techniques, and books on regression therapy for those who wish to go deeper into therapeutic work.

How to Use This Course

Watch each lesson, then practice with a partner. Do not move to the next lesson until you can reliably reproduce the technique from the previous one. Hypnosis is learned through repetition and hands-on experience, not through intellectual understanding alone. If a technique does not work on your first attempt, try again — adjust your voice, your pacing, your wording. Every subject is different, and the skill lies in adapting the technique to the person in front of you.

🧠 Key Takeaway: Hypnosis is learned by doing, not by reading. Practice each technique until it becomes automatic before moving on to the next lesson. Skill comes from repetition, not theory.

📝 Your First Task

Assignment 1: Read the first chapter of Dave Elman's Hypnotherapy. Pay attention to how Elman structures his pre-talk and how direct his language is with his subjects. Notice the pacing — how he builds expectation before each suggestion.

Assignment 2: Practice the Pre-talk

Your goal is to explain hypnosis in under two minutes using natural, conversational language. Do not use jargon. Do not lecture. Simply describe the experience they are about to have. Use this script as a starting point:

"Hypnosis is simply focused attention. You have experienced it before — when you were so absorbed in a book or a movie that the world around you faded away. I am going to help you do that intentionally. There is nothing to fear and nothing to resist. You remain in control at all times. Your mind will do exactly what it naturally does when it focuses completely on one thing, and that is hypnosis."

Assignment 3: Record yourself delivering the pre-talk and listen back. Notice where you hesitate, where you rush, where your voice drops. A smooth, confident delivery builds trust. Practice until the words feel natural.

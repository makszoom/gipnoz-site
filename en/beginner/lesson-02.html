About This Lesson

This is the foundational vocabulary lesson of the entire course. Kein defines the core concepts you'll use every time you work with a subject: rapport, somnambulism, suggestibility, waking hypnosis, and the subconscious mind. If you understand these, everything else falls into place.

Kein also makes a bold statement that defines his entire teaching philosophy: he refuses to work with anyone outside of somnambulism. Light and medium trance states are useful only as stepping stones. The goal is always deep trance — and it's easier to reach than most people think.

Psychosomatics & the Mind-Body Connection

Kein starts with psychosomatics to illustrate how real the mind-body link is. Skin conditions like eczema, asthma, and many other illnesses have a psychosomatic component. Modern medicine acknowledges that a high percentage of all disease has some psychosomatic origin — which is precisely where hypnosis works.

Rapport — The Relationship

Rapport is simply a working relationship. But in hypnosis, it's intimate — you're going into someone's mind. If you don't like the person, or they don't like you, nothing will work. Kein's advice: if you sense a mismatch, refer them elsewhere. It's not failure, it's professionalism.

Resistance vs. Fear

A person who doesn't enter trance is NOT resisting. They have an unaddressed fear or a block. Never tell a client "you're resisting" — they'll feel blamed and shut down. True resistance only happens when someone is in your office against their will (forced by a spouse, parent, or court). Everyone else wants to go into trance but may not know how yet.

Somnambulism — The Gold Standard

Somnambulism is the third level of trance — deep enough for all therapeutic work. The old statistics say 20% of people reach this on their first visit. Kein rejects this. In his classroom, he starts at somnambulism and works from there. His philosophy: if you can reach gold, why settle for bronze? Everything possible in lighter states is also possible in somnambulism — but the reverse is not true. Regression, pain control, and anesthesia all require deep trance.

The Subconscious Mind

Kein doesn't pretend to know exactly where the subconscious resides — "it might be partly in your big toe," he jokes. What matters is that it's a level of processing separate from conscious awareness. It's the part that responds to suggestion once the critical factor is bypassed.

Waking Hypnosis

Waking hypnosis is giving hypnotic suggestions while the person believes they're in their normal conscious state. An intelligent hypnotist can hypnotize someone during the pre-talk itself — addressing fears, building expectation — and do a third of the work before any formal induction even begins. Kein tells a story of a doctor who inserted a chest tube into a patient using only waking hypnosis — no formal trance, no anesthesia, no pain.

Post-Hypnotic Suggestions

A post-hypnotic suggestion is given during trance but takes effect after the subject comes out. Kein shares a funny (and cautionary) story: during a stage show, he gave a man a suggestion that whenever he heard the word "banana," he'd see a pit bull chasing him and run in circles. He forgot to cancel it. The next day, the man's wife asked him in a grocery store, "Honey, do you want bananas with your cereal?" — and he started running through the aisles.

The lesson: always cancel any suggestion that you don't intend to be permanent. In therapeutic work this isn't usually an issue, but in demonstrations and stage work, be meticulous.

🧠 Key Takeaway: Somnambulism is the target state for all serious work. Don't settle for light trance. And start doing your work during the pre-talk — waking hypnosis can accomplish more than most beginners realize.

📝 Practice: Build Rapport

Find a partner and practice the pre-talk. Sit close, maintain eye contact, and speak about hypnosis naturally. The goal is not to induce trance — just to build enough comfort that the person feels safe. Notice when they relax their posture and their breathing changes. That's the beginning of rapport.

Pre-talk Opening

"Hypnosis is a completely natural state. You've been in it before — when you were so absorbed in a movie you didn't hear someone call your name, or when you were driving and realized you don't remember the last five miles. I'm just going to help you get there intentionally. There's nothing to fear and nothing to resist."

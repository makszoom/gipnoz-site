B02 — Core Concepts: Rapport, Somnambulism & Suggestibility

~50 minutes

About This Lesson

This lesson defines the three foundational concepts every hypnotist must understand: rapport (the working relationship with the subject), somnambulism (the deep trance level where all therapeutic work happens), and suggestibility (how to measure and increase it). It also covers the pre-talk, resistance versus fear, and the technique of waking hypnosis — giving suggestive instructions while the subject believes they are in normal consciousness.

If you understand these concepts, everything else in hypnosis falls into place. They are the vocabulary you will use every single time you work with a subject. The operating philosophy throughout this course is that light and medium trance states are useful only as stepping stones to somnambulism — the goal is always deep trance, and it is easier to reach than most beginners realize.

Rapport — The Foundation

Rapport is simply a working relationship between the hypnotist and the subject. In hypnosis, this relationship is intimate — you are going into someone's mind. Without rapport, nothing works. Build it through mirroring the subject's posture and speech patterns, matching their tone of voice, and demonstrating genuine interest in their experience. If you genuinely do not like the subject, or sense a fundamental mismatch, refer them to another hypnotist. This is not failure — it is professionalism.

Resistance vs. Fear

A subject who does not enter trance is not necessarily resisting. More often, they are afraid — afraid of losing control, afraid of failing, afraid of what they might discover. Never tell a subject "you are resisting me." This creates blame and shuts down cooperation. True resistance only occurs when someone is in your office against their will, coerced by a spouse, parent, or court. Everyone else wants to go into trance but may not know how. The pre-talk, when done correctly, eliminates ninety percent of this fear before the induction even begins.

Somnambulism — The Gold Standard

Somnambulism is the third level of trance — deep enough for all therapeutic work. It is characterized by amnesia (the subject may not remember what happened during trance), spontaneous anesthesia (numbness occurs without direct suggestion), and literal interpretation of suggestions (the subconscious takes words at face value). The approach in this course is to work exclusively in somnambulism. Everything possible in lighter states is also possible in somnambulism, but the reverse is not true — regression, pain control, and anesthesia all require deep trance. Light and medium trance are useful only as the path to reach it.

Waking Hypnosis

Waking hypnosis is the technique of giving hypnotic suggestions while the subject believes they are in their normal conscious state. An experienced hypnotist can begin working during the pre-talk itself — addressing fears, building expectation, planting the seeds of the therapeutic suggestions — and accomplish up to one-third of the work before any formal induction begins. The subject's critical factor is still active during waking hypnosis, so the suggestions must be framed naturally, as if they are ordinary conversation. This is a skill that improves with practice: learning to embed therapeutic instructions in everyday language.

🧠 Key Takeaway: Somnambulism is the gold standard for all therapeutic work. Do not settle for light trance. Use the pre-talk to build rapport, eliminate fear, and begin waking hypnosis before the formal induction.

📝 Practice: Pre-talk & Rapport Building

Find a willing partner and practice the pre-talk in three different variants. The goal is not to induce trance — just to build enough comfort that the person feels safe and understands what is about to happen. Notice when their posture relaxes and their breathing changes. That is the beginning of rapport.

Variant 1: Rapport Builder (Standard Pre-talk)

Build immediate trust by explaining hypnosis as a familiar, natural state — this removes fear of the unknown and normalizes the experience before you begin.

"Hypnosis is a completely natural state. You have been in it before — absorbed in a movie, lost in thought while driving, so focused on a book that you did not hear someone call your name. I am simply going to help you get there intentionally. There is nothing to fear and nothing to resist. Your mind will do exactly what it naturally does when it relaxes."

Variant 2: Pre-talk for Analytical Clients

Analytical types need to feel in control. Emphasize that they will observe the process clearly and nothing happens without their permission.

"Your mind is highly analytical, and that is good — it means you will notice the process clearly and understand exactly what is happening at each step. I am not going to make you do anything. I will simply guide, and your mind will do what it naturally does when it allows itself to relax. You remain in control at all times. You can speak if you need to, open your eyes if you choose to. Nothing happens without your permission."

Variant 3: Explaining Somnambulism

When your subject needs to understand what deep trance feels like before going there, use this gentle, reassuring explanation.

"You know that state just before falling asleep when you are still aware of your surroundings but your body is deeply relaxed? That is similar to what we are aiming for. You will hear everything I say. You can speak if needed. But your body will be completely at rest, and your mind will be open and receptive — focused inward rather than outward. That is the state where real change happens."

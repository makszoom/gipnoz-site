B03 — Bypassing the Critical Factor & Emergency Anesthesia

~45 minutes

About This Lesson

The critical factor is the mind's filter that evaluates incoming suggestions and rejects anything it does not accept. Every hypnotist's job is to bypass this filter. Once bypassed, the subconscious accepts suggestions directly — including suggestions for complete anesthesia. This lesson covers the mechanism of the critical factor, the "pretending game" technique for instant eye catalepsy and bypass, acceptable selective thinking as a protective frame, and the application of these techniques as an emergency anesthesia protocol.

The pretending game is one of the fastest and most reliable methods for bypassing the critical factor. It works because the instruction to "pretend" is not recognized by the critical factor as a hypnotic suggestion — it is recognized as a game, an experiment. The critical factor steps aside to witness what happens next, and in that moment, the bypass is complete.

What Is the Critical Factor

The critical factor is the gatekeeper between the conscious and subconscious mind. It evaluates every incoming suggestion against the subject's stored beliefs and experiences. If a suggestion matches what the subject already believes, it passes through to the subconscious. If it conflicts with existing beliefs, it is rejected. Bypassing the critical factor means giving instructions that it does not recognize as suggestions at all — instructions to pretend, to imagine, to test. When framed this way, the critical factor has nothing to reject because it does not perceive a threat.

How to Bypass: The Pretending Game

Ask the subject to close their eyes and pretend that their eyelids are glued shut. Tell them to pretend with full force, the way they did as a child when imagination felt completely real. When they try to open their eyes and cannot (because they are pretending so hard), the critical factor steps aside. Use this exact script:

"Close your eyes. Now pretend — pretend as hard as you can — that your eyelids are glued shut. Imagine someone poured superglue on them and they are sealed tight. Pretend the way you did as a child when you played make-believe and it felt completely real. Your eyelids are fixed, sealed, locked together. Now try to open them. Try. You will find that the harder you try, the tighter they stay shut. That is it — your eyes are locked. Go ahead, try again. Good. Now stop trying and just relax."

When the subject tries and cannot open their eyes, the critical factor steps aside: "I cannot open them — let us see what happens next." At that moment, the bypass is complete. The subject is now in a suggestible state without having gone through any formal induction. This technique produces eye catalepsy in under a minute on the first attempt with most subjects.

Acceptable Selective Thinking

Once the critical factor is bypassed, the next step is to install a protective frame. This ensures the subconscious accepts all subsequent suggestions without resistance. Use this script immediately after achieving eye catalepsy:

"Nothing I do here will bother or disturb you in any way. Nothing will cause you any discomfort. Everything that happens will be perfectly acceptable to your mind. Your subconscious will accept each suggestion easily and naturally, because it knows you are safe and in control."

The word "nothing" is emphasized — it is a hypnotic command delivered under the subject's conscious radar. Because the critical factor is already out of the way, this suggestion goes directly to the subconscious and acts as blanket permission for all subsequent instructions.

Emergency Anesthesia Protocol

Step 1: Bypass the critical factor using the pretending game. Step 2: Install acceptable selective thinking. Step 3: Redirect the subject's awareness away from the target area. Use this script to create numbness:

"Now focus all your attention on your [left/right] hand. Notice the exact sensation of the air against your skin. That feeling of pressure, of warmth, of your hand being exactly where it is. Now that same feeling is spreading to your [target area]. Not sharpness — just pressure. Only pressure. Like someone pressing through a thick leather glove. Tell me when the numbness is complete. Just nod when it feels completely different from the rest of your body."

When executed correctly, the subject feels pressure but no pain. This protocol works for dental work, minor medical procedures, and crisis situations where chemical anesthesia is not available. In emergency settings, the critical factor is already weakened by shock, making it easier to bypass. The key is to act before the patient has time to panic.

🧠 Key Takeaway: Do not fight the critical factor — bypass it. The pretending game works because the critical factor steps aside to witness the experiment. Once bypassed, the subconscious accepts suggestions directly, including suggestions for complete anesthesia.

📝 Practice: Bypassing the Critical Factor

Try these techniques with a willing partner. In all cases, if the subject's eyes stayed closed when they tried to open them, you have successfully bypassed the critical factor. No needles are required for these practice exercises — use a gentle pinch to test anesthesia.

Variant 1: Eye Catalepsy via Pretending

This is the primary bypass technique from the lesson. Use the full pretending game script to achieve eye catalepsy in under one minute.

"Close your eyes. Pretend your eyelids are glued shut. Superglue — the strongest kind. Pretend so hard that when you try to open them, they simply will not move. Keep pretending — your eyes are fixed, sealed, locked. Now try to open them. Try. You will find they will not open. The harder you try, the tighter they stay. Good. Stop trying now and just relax deeply."

Test: ask them to try. If they cannot, the bypass is working. Proceed immediately to acceptable selective thinking.

Variant 2: Hand Anesthesia

After achieving eye catalepsy and installing acceptable selective thinking, move the numbness to a body part. This is the gateway to full surgical anesthesia.

"Now pretend your hand is wrapped in thick leather. Thick, heavy leather. You can feel pressure, but nothing sharp. Nothing sharp at all. I am going to touch your hand, and you will notice only pressure — like someone pressing through a thick glove. No sharpness, no pain. Just pressure. Just the sensation of touch without any discomfort. Let me know when that feels right."

Test with a gentle pinch on the hand. Ask the subject to rate any sensation on a scale of one to ten. If they report anything below three, the anesthesia is working.

Variant 3: Quick Anesthesia for Dental Simulation

Practice this rapid numbing script for dental or medical scenarios where speed matters. The countdown creates expectation and deepens the response.

"Fix your mind on this suggestion: as I count to three, all feeling in your jaw will disappear completely. Like novocaine spreading, but you control it — your mind controls it completely. One — the numbness begins, spreading through your jaw like a gentle wave. Two — deeper now, spreading further, the numbness grows. Three — complete. Your jaw is completely numb. Test it with your fingernail — press gently. You will feel pressure but no sharp sensation at all."

The subject should feel their fingernail pressing but no sharp sensation. If they do, the suggestion is working. If not, repeat the countdown with more vivid imagery.

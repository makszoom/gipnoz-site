B04 — Trance Levels & Coma State Management

~45 minutes

About This Lesson

Trance occurs on a spectrum — from light (where the subject feels relaxed but may doubt they were hypnotized) through deep somnambulism (where amnesia, catalepsy, and spontaneous anesthesia occur) down to the coma state (complete physical shutdown). This lesson maps those levels and teaches you how to recognize and handle each one, including the rare case where standard emergence procedures don't work.

The Levels of Trance

Level 1-2 (Light): The subject experiences physical relaxation and eyelash flutter. They may say "I wasn't hypnotized" because they were aware of everything. Ideomotor responses (finger twitches, eye movements) are present.

Level 3 (Medium): Catalepsy becomes possible — the subject can hold a limb in an uncomfortable position without effort. Partial amnesia may occur. The subject accepts suggestions more readily.

Level 4-5 (Somnambulism): Complete amnesia is common. Spontaneous anesthesia occurs — the subject feels no discomfort. They respond literally to suggestions (e.g., a hand on the head feels like a brick). Hallucinations (positive and negative) are possible.

Below Somnambulism — Coma State: Complete catalepsy — no voluntary movement. Breathing slows dramatically. Time distortion occurs — what feels like seconds may be 20+ minutes. Standard emergence counting may not produce a response.

Recognizing Coma

The coma state has distinct physical signs: breathing drops to 1-2 breaths per minute. The skin may appear pale or gray. The eyes roll upward. The subject does not respond to normal emergence counting (1-to-5). Time distortion is extreme — the subject may emerge believing only seconds passed when it has been many minutes.

Safe Emergence from Deep Trance

If standard counting (1-5) does not bring the subject out, follow this protocol step by step. Each step has its own script.

Step 1: Give Reassurance

Prevent panic — in both you and the subject — by calmly acknowledging the depth of the state.

"You are safe. You are in a very deep state of relaxation, and that is perfectly fine. There is no rush. I am going to help you come back slowly and comfortably. Just listen to my voice and follow along."

Step 2: Progressive Emergence

Break the emergence into stages rather than demanding full return at once. This gives the mind an intermediate waypoint.

"On 3 you will rise to a lighter state — somnambulism — while staying deeply relaxed. One ... two ... three — that is it, you can hear my voice clearly now. On 5 you will be fully awake and alert. One ... two ... three ... four ... five — open your eyes now, fully awake and refreshed."

Step 3: Physical Anchor

A light touch on the shoulder with gentle pressure provides orientation to the real world. Combine it with verbal cues.

"Feel the pressure on your shoulder. That is the real world. Focus on that sensation — it is solid, it is real. Come back toward it. As I count to 5, follow that feeling back to full awareness. One — feeling the pressure. Two — becoming more aware. Three — taking a deep breath. Four — energy returning. Five — eyes open, fully awake."

Step 4: The Fright Command (last resort only)

Only when all other methods have failed and the subject appears genuinely stuck. Use a firm, commanding voice — never as a first approach.

"Open your eyes now! Take a deep breath. You are fully awake and alert. Look around the room. You are safe and completely in control."

When to Refer

If a subject consistently goes into states you cannot manage — such as repeatedly dropping into coma without being prompted — refer them to a more experienced practitioner. The coma state is safe when handled properly, but it requires confidence and experience. Know your limits.

🧠 Key Takeaway: Know the levels. Don't panic in deep trance — progressive emergence always works. The coma state is safe when you know how to handle it.

📝 Practice: Emergence Protocols

Practice these three variants until they are automatic. Each serves a different purpose — know which one to use and when.

Variant 1: Standard Emergence from Deep Trance

This is your default emergence script for routine sessions. Use it when the subject is in deep somnambulism and ready to return.

"In a moment I will count from 1 to 5. On 5, your eyes will open, and you will feel refreshed and alert. One — beginning to surface, becoming aware of the room around you. Two — more aware, feeling sensation return to your body. Three — take a deep breath, filling your lungs with energy. Four — feeling energy return throughout your entire body. Five — eyes open, wide awake, feeling wonderful."

Variant 2: Progressive Emergence (for Stuck Subjects)

When the standard count does not produce a response, use staged emergence. This gives the mind an intermediate destination before full return.

"You are in a very deep place. That is fine — there is no rush. On 3, you will rise to a lighter state but stay deeply relaxed and comfortable. One ... two ... three — that is it, you can hear my voice clearly now. On 5, I want you to open your eyes and return fully. One — becoming aware. Two — feeling your body. Three — taking a deep breath. Four — energy returning. Five — open your eyes now, fully alert and refreshed."

Variant 3: Trance Depth Check

Use this during a session to measure the subject's depth without breaking trance. The finger signal gives you a real-time read on their state.

"I am going to count from 1 to 10. Raise your finger when I reach the number that matches how deep you feel. One means wide awake, ten means the deepest you have ever been. Just let your finger lift naturally when you hear the right number. One ... two ... three ... four ... five ... six ... seven ... eight ... nine ... ten."

Practice the depth check regularly — it gives you a real-time measure of your subject's state without breaking trance. Use it before, during, and after therapeutic work to confirm depth.

About This Lesson

Deepening is what separates a light trance from the deep somnambulistic state where real therapeutic work happens. Kein teaches multiple deepening methods in this lesson, emphasizing that no single technique works for everyone — you need a toolkit of approaches and the judgment to choose the right one.

The lesson covers counting techniques, hallucination inductions (staircase, beach), ideomotor suggestions, and the "pyramid" method — stacking multiple inductions on top of each other to reach deeper levels. Throughout, Kein stresses the importance of speaking to the subject like a best friend.

Counting Techniques

The simplest deepening method is reverse counting. Kein teaches a critical nuance: adjust your counting to the subject's breathing. If they breathe slowly, count slowly between their breaths. If they breathe quickly, group numbers together ("seven and six, five and four"). The counting should feel natural, not mechanical. If the subject and counting are out of sync, the deepening won't work.

Hallucination Techniques

For visual subjects, Kein uses hallucination deepening. Ask them to imagine themselves at the top of a staircase, and each step down takes them deeper. Or lying on a beach watching clouds drift by. The key is to establish that the location is safe during the pre-talk — don't put someone in a situation that triggers anxiety. These are guided visualizations that bypass the critical factor through sensory immersion.

Speaking Like a Best Friend

Kein tells a story about comforting a heartbroken friend. The tone, the patience, the complete acceptance — that's exactly how you should speak to someone in hypnosis. "If you get that kind of rapport with yourself," he says, "you'll speak perfectly. You'll naturally adjust to the person's personality." Your voice should convey that you are on their side, that nothing they experience will surprise or bother you, and that they are safe to go as deep as they need to.

The Pyramid Technique

Pyramiding means using one induction to create a baseline, then layering another induction on top to go deeper. For example: start with eye catalepsy, then add progressive relaxation, then add a countdown. Each layer drives the subject deeper. The Elman induction itself is a form of pyramiding — it stacks physical relaxation, mental relaxation, and eye catalepsy in sequence.

🧠 Key Takeaway: Deepening is not a single technique — it's a skill of reading your subject and choosing the right tool. Count with their breath. Use imagery they can connect with. Speak with the warmth of a friend. And don't be afraid to layer techniques.

📝 Practice: Counting Deepening

Practice the adjusted counting technique. Have a partner go into a light trance, then deepen them using reverse counting, pacing your count to their exhale.

Staircase Deepening

"Imagine yourself at the top of a beautiful staircase. It's safe, familiar, comfortable. In a moment I'll ask you to begin walking down. Each step down takes you deeper into relaxation. Ten... feel the step beneath your foot. Nine... deeper down. Eight... more relaxed with each step. Seven... six... all the way down to a place of complete peace."

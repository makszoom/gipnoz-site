B11 — Pain Control & Anesthesia

~45 minutes

🎬 Video will be added after upload to YouTube

About This Lesson

Hypnotic anesthesia works by redirecting attention. The mind can only focus on one thing at a time. If you fully engage the subject's attention on eye catalepsy or another sensation, pain signals cannot reach conscious awareness simultaneously. This technique was used to reduce surgical anesthesia by up to 75%.

The core insight: the human mind can only focus on one thing at a time. If you engage the subject's attention fully on eye catalepsy, their brain cannot process pain signals simultaneously. This is not a relaxation technique — it is a cognitive redirection technique.

The Principle: One Thought at a Time

The human mind processes one conscious thought at a time (switching rapidly). If you engage the subject in eye catalepsy — their brain is occupied. Pain signals arrive but aren't processed. This is not pain suppression — it is attention redirection.

The Anesthesia Induction Protocol

(1) Achieve somnambulism via standard Elman induction. (2) Guide the numbness into the target area. (3) Test by gentle pinch. (4) Reinforce with a post-hypnotic suggestion.

"Now send that relaxation into your [target area]. It's going numb. Like novocaine spreading. You'll feel pressure but no discomfort."

"This feeling will last as long as needed."

Pain Control Technique (The Pinch Method)

After establishing anesthesia, teach the subject self-control: (1) Bring pain back briefly. (2) Ask them to pinch their fingers — pain disappears. (3) Repeat 15 times. (4) Install the self-control suggestion.

"Now you control the discomfort — it doesn't control you."

Chronic Pain

For ongoing conditions, teach self-hypnosis with the pinch technique. The subject can activate anesthesia whenever needed. Important: require medical supervision for undiagnosed pain — pain is a signal.

🧠 Key Takeaway: Anesthesia is attention redirection, not pain suppression. The Elman eye catalepsy is the gateway — once the eyes are locked, you can generalize numbness to any body part.

📝 Practice: Hand Anesthesia

With a partner, use the eye catalepsy induction to create anesthesia in one hand. Test by pinching (gently!) — first the non-anesthetized hand, then the anesthetized hand. The difference should be noticeable.

Anesthesia Induction

"Close your eyes. Relax your eyelids until they stop working. Test them. They're locked. Now send that feeling of relaxation down through your entire body... but especially into your right hand. Your right hand is going numb. Like Novocaine spreading through it. It feels thick, heavy, distant. When I touch it, you'll feel pressure but no discomfort. Your hand is completely protected."

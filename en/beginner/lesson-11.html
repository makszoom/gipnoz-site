About This Lesson

This lesson tells the remarkable story of how Dave Elman taught the Mayo brothers his anesthesia technique — and how they used it to reduce surgical anesthesia drug dosages by 75% while achieving deeper anesthesia. Kein then demonstrates the technique in class and breaks down exactly why it works.

The core insight: the human mind can only focus on one thing at a time. If you engage the subject's attention fully on eye catalepsy, their brain cannot process pain signals simultaneously. This is not a relaxation technique — it's a cognitive redirection technique.

The Mayo Brothers Story

Elman met the Mayo brothers and taught them his anesthesia technique. The protocol: visit the patient the night before surgery, teach them eye catalepsy ("close your eyes and relax them until they stop working — test them"), and tell them they'll do the same thing in the morning before surgery. The next day, the anesthesiologist guides them through the same eye catalepsy — then reduces the drug dosage by 25%, then 50%, then 75%. At 75% reduction, the patient was MORE anesthetized than with full drugs alone.

One anesthesiologist from New Jersey who attended Kein's training took this to extremes: he wanted to see how far he could reduce the dose. He got down to 25% of normal — achieving deeper anesthesia with less chemical. He was too afraid to go to 0%, but Kein suggests it's possible. The key is that the eye catalepsy engages the mind so completely that pain signals cannot reach conscious awareness.

How It Works

The mechanism is simple: the conscious mind can only process one thing at a time. When you focus completely on keeping your eyes relaxed and non-functional, there's no bandwidth left for pain. This is why the Elman induction is so effective for pain control — it's built into the first step of the induction itself.

For chronic pain management, the same principle applies. Teach the patient a self-hypnosis trigger (like the finger drop) that they can activate whenever pain arises. The trigger redirects attention away from pain and into a state where the subconscious can block the pain signal.

🧠 Key Takeaway: Pain control through hypnosis isn't about relaxation — it's about attention management. The mind can only process one primary signal at a time. Engage it fully on eye catalepsy, and pain cannot register. This is the most practical medical application of hypnosis.

📝 Practice: Hand Anesthesia

With a partner, use the eye catalepsy induction to create anesthesia in one hand. Test by pinching (gently!) — first the non-anesthetized hand, then the anesthetized hand. The difference should be noticeable.

Anesthesia Induction

"Close your eyes. Relax your eyelids until they stop working. Test them. They're locked. Now send that feeling of relaxation down through your entire body... but especially into your right hand. Your right hand is going numb. Like Novocaine spreading through it. It feels thick, heavy, distant. When I touch it, you'll feel pressure but no discomfort. Your hand is completely protected."

1.1 — Hypnotic Contract and Preparation

Module 1 • ~25 minutes

📜 Hypnotic Contract

Hypnosis is not magic and not one person's power over another. It is an agreement. Explicit or implicit, but it is always there. Without it, hypnosis does not work.

The essence of the contract is simple: the client agrees to follow your instructions, and you take responsibility for their state. You speak directly and honestly:

"I can help you, if you follow my instructions. If you do not follow my instructions, I cannot help you."

This phrase does two things at once: it establishes your competence (you know what you're doing) and transfers responsibility to the client (the result depends on their willingness to cooperate).

Without an explicit or implicit contract, any induction will run into internal resistance. The client's subconscious doesn't know whether to trust the operator — and puts up blocks.

🧠 Key point: The contract can be nonverbal. When a person sits in the chair and closes their eyes at your request — they have already agreed. But verbalizing the contract multiplies compliance. Always say it out loud.

🚫 Four Forbidden Words

In the pre-talk (conversation before induction), there are words that must not be spoken. They activate unwanted associations in the client's subconscious. Here they are:

1. "Under hypnosis"

The phrase "under hypnosis" creates an image in the client's mind of a passive, will-less state in which they will "lose control." This breeds fear. Instead, say: "in the hypnotic state" or simply "in a state of relaxation."

2. "Wake up"

"Wake up" — an association with sleep. Hypnosis is not sleep, but a state of heightened suggestibility. A client told to "wake up" may start associating hypnosis with loss of consciousness. Use: "return to your usual state," "open your eyes," "come out of trance."

3. "Try"

The word "try" implies the possibility of failure. When you say "try to open your eyes," the subconscious hears: "it might not work." In hypnosis, we only say what will happen: "and when you are ready, your eyes will open." The difference in wording is the difference in outcome.

4. "Hope"

"Hope" is another word of doubt. "I hope this works" is heard by the subconscious as "I'm not sure this will work." The hypnotist speaks with certainty: "this will work," "this is happening." No hoping — only statements of fact.

🧠 Rule: Words of doubt and uncertainty kill trance. Words of confidence and specificity deepen it. Watch your speech: every word either works for hypnosis or against it.

✅ Three Permissions Before Induction

Before starting the induction, you must obtain three explicit permissions from the client. This is part of the hypnotic contract and the foundation of compliance.

1. Permission to use first name / informal address

Ask: "Do you mind if I call you by your first name? In hypnosis, it helps with relaxation." Using a first name in trance removes the barrier of formality and strengthens rapport.

2. Permission to touch the arm, shoulder, or forehead

Say: "During the process, I may lightly touch your arm or shoulder to deepen relaxation. Is that okay?" Touches work as anchors — they reinforce the state. But without permission, any touch may be perceived as a boundary violation and destroy trust.

3. A readiness signal

Agree on a signal: "If you feel uncomfortable or want to stop — just raise your hand or say 'stop.'" This gives the client a sense of control. Paradoxically, it is the feeling of control that allows them to relax more deeply.

🤝 Compliance — Readiness to Follow

Compliance is the conscious willingness of the client to follow your instructions. It is not hypnosis. Compliance is the door through which hypnosis enters.

The difference is simple:

  • Compliance — the client consciously decides to follow instructions because they trust you and want results
  • Hypnosis — the client's subconscious automatically responds to suggestions without conscious control

Compliance is built in the pre-talk. If you haven't gained compliance — hypnosis won't work, because the client will resist on a conscious level. Before inducing trance, make sure the client is ready to cooperate.

🧠 Compliance check: Ask the client to do something simple — extend their hand, take a deep breath. If they do it without resistance — you have compliance. If they hesitate or ask "why?" — go back to building the contract.

🎙️ Hypnotic Voice

Many beginners think the hypnotic voice should be monotonous, like a robot's. This is a mistake. A monotonous voice puts people to sleep, but doesn't induce trance.

The hypnotic voice is congruent. This means your intonation matches the content of your words. If you say "relaxation," your voice sounds relaxed. If you say "deeper," your voice becomes slightly lower and slower.

Speech speed induces trance. When you slow down your pace — you set the rhythm for the client's subconscious. It tunes into your tempo and slows down too. This is a physiological mechanism: the brain synchronizes with the speaker's rhythm.

Practical techniques:

  • Speak slightly slower than usual — but not so slow that it sounds unnatural
  • Pause 2–3 seconds between key phrases — this gives the subconscious time to "digest" the suggestion
  • Watch your volume: a quiet voice makes the client lean in, narrowing their attention
  • Vary your intonation: drop your voice at the end of each suggestive phrase (descending intonation works like a command)

🧠 Exercise: Read any paragraph from this lesson aloud at half your normal speed. Record it. Listen back — does it sound comfortable? It should feel like a calm, confident voice, without tension.

⚡ Abreaction and How to Handle It

Abreaction is an emotional outburst that can occur in trance. The client may start crying, trembling, showing anxiety or fear. This is normal — it's the subconscious releasing suppressed emotions.

Your job is not to panic, but to handle the abreaction skillfully. A three-step algorithm:

1. Keep a calm tone

Your voice is an anchor of safety. If you start speaking faster or higher — the client will sense your anxiety and the abreaction will intensify. Speak evenly, slowly, confidently. Your calmness transfers to the client.

2. Don't touch the client

During abreaction, do not touch the person (unless agreed upon beforehand). Any unexpected touch may be perceived as a threat and intensify the reaction. Give space.

3. Use the phrase: "It all fades away, focus on your breathing"

Say calmly: "Everything you feel — just fades away. Focus on your breathing. Inhale and exhale. With every exhale, it all drifts away." Repeat this in an even tone until the emotion subsides. Breathing is the fastest way to return the client to a neutral state.

🧠 Important: Abreaction is not a failure. It's a sign that the subconscious is actively working. If you calmly guide the client through the abreaction, your rapport will become stronger and the trance deeper. Don't interrupt the session — see it through to completion.

👁️ Physiological Signs of Trance

How do you know a person is truly in trance? Look for these signs — they are objective and independent of what the client says:

  • Flushed cheeks — capillary dilation, blood rush to the face. A reliable sign of deep relaxation
  • Eyelash flutter — fine, rapid twitching of closed eyelids. Indicates uneven relaxation of the eye muscles
  • Eye moistening — tearing without crying. The eyelids stop closing completely, moistening the cornea
  • Body warmth — the client may say they feel hot. This is vasodilation and increased blood flow
  • Horizontal eye movement — under closed lids, the eyeballs move side to side. A sign of rapid subconscious scanning (REM-like activity)

Memorize these signs — they are your feedback. If you see them, the technique is working — deepen the state. If you don't see them, the client may not be in trance yet — go back to establishing the contract and compliance.

🪑 Body Position

Proper posture is the foundation of successful induction. Before trance begins, ask the client to sit as follows:

  • Feet flat on the floor — both feet fully touching the floor. Not crossed, not tucked. Grounding gives a sense of stability and safety
  • Hands uncrossed — hands resting freely on the lap or armrests. Crossed arms are a "closed posture," a nonverbal signal of defense and resistance
  • Back straight but not tense — slouching makes breathing difficult, while an overly straight back creates tension
  • Head slightly tilted forward or resting on a headrest — this allows the neck to be fully relaxed

If the client has crossed their arms or legs — politely ask: "Place your hands on your knees, just loosely. That way relaxation will come easier." Don't command — suggest.

🧠 Observation: Notice — when you ask someone to change their posture (uncross arms, put feet on the floor), and they comply, this is already a compliance test. If they do it without question — you're ready for induction.

📝 Practical Assignment

Goal: Practice the pre-talk and establishing the hypnotic contract on 3 different people.

  1. Choose a partner and seat them in a comfortable chair. Check body position: feet on the floor, hands uncrossed.
  2. Deliver the hypnotic contract: "I can help you, if you follow my instructions. If you do not follow my instructions, I cannot help you."
  3. Obtain the three permissions: use first name/informal address, touch shoulder/arm/forehead, readiness signal (raise hand or say "stop").
  4. Check compliance — ask them to do a simple action (deep breath, raise hand).
  5. Practice the hypnotic voice: read any paragraph from this lesson at a slower pace. Record it.
  6. Repeat with 3 different people. Write down: how each reacted, any questions or resistance, how you explained the contract.

🧠 Important: Do not move to trance induction until you feel you have gained compliance. Compliance is the foundation. If the foundation is weak — the whole building will collapse. Better to spend 10 minutes on pre-talk than to fail the session in the first minute.

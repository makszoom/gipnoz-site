1.2 — Eyelid Catalepsy and Body Scan

Module 1 • ~20 minutes

🎬 Video will be inserted after upload to YouTube

🧩 First Wedge into the Subconscious — A Pre-Hypnosis Phenomenon

Most beginner hypnotists make a mistake: they first induce hypnosis (eyes close, body relaxes), and then try to create a phenomenon (catalepsy, amnesia, hallucination). Elman reverses this order.

We first create the phenomenon — and from the phenomenon, hypnosis emerges. This is Elman's shortcut. No need to spend five minutes coaxing the client to relax. Just give one suggestion that the subconscious must accept — and trance arises automatically.

The phrase that does this:

"Relax your eyelids as deeply as possible — so deeply that they will not open."

This suggestion defies common sense. In everyday life, eyelids always open. And suddenly the hypnotist says: "Relax them so they won't open." The client's subconscious cannot verify this statement — it doesn't know what level of relaxation is sufficient. So it bypasses the critical factor and accepts the suggestion as a possible reality.

🧠 Key point: A suggestion that cannot be consciously verified at the moment it is received is the shortest route to bypassing the critical factor. "Relax your eyelids so deeply that they will not open" is the perfect first wedge. The client cannot say "I already did that" or "It's not working." They simply continue relaxing.

👁️ Eyelid Catalepsy — The First Test

After you give the suggestion for eyelid relaxation, you test whether it worked. This is the first test of compliance and the initial level of hypnosis. The test is performed with these words:

"Maintaining that deep relaxation in your eyelids, test them and make sure they won't open. Move your eyebrows, wrinkle your forehead. Feel how your eyelids stay relaxed and closed."

When you give this instruction, something important happens: you give the client permission not to open their eyes. In everyday life, if someone is told "close your eyes," they perceive it as a temporary action. But when you add "and make sure they won't open," the subconscious shifts gears: the task changes from "open/close" to "maintain this state."

⚠️ If the Client Opens Their Eyes

Some clients, in response to "test that they won't open," simply open their eyes. This is normal, especially in early practice. Your response:

"You just saw that they open. You need to relax them to the point where they won't open. Close your eyes and keep relaxing your eyelids. Deeper and deeper…"

You don't scold the client. You use their action as feedback and redirect: "You saw they open — that means the relaxation isn't deep enough yet. Keep going."

The eyelid catalepsy test is the first milestone. If the client keeps their eyes closed and cannot open them, it means:

  • Compliance is present — the client is following instructions
  • The initial level of hypnosis has been reached — the subconscious has accepted the first suggestion
  • You can move to the next step — the wave of relaxation through the body

🌊 Body Scan Relaxation Wave

Once eyelid catalepsy is achieved, you need to spread this relaxation throughout the entire body. Instruction:

"Now take that feeling of relaxation from your eyelids and fill your whole body with it. Send it like a wave — from the top of your head to the tips of your fingers and toes. Fill every cell, every muscle. Let it flow downward — through your face, neck, shoulders, arms, chest, stomach, back, hips, legs, feet… Your whole body relaxes with that same deep wave."

The connection is direct: the same relaxation felt in the eyelids spreads throughout the body. The client doesn't need to search for relaxation anew — they simply transfer an already familiar sensation. This creates a feeling of continuity and depth.

When the wave reaches the feet, you can add detail:

"Every muscle in your body is now relaxed. You can feel warmth spreading through your whole body. Relaxation deepens with every exhale…"

🧠 Technical note: Elman's body scan is not just relaxation. It is deepening of the first phenomenon. When the client has confirmed that their eyelids won't open, and now relaxes their whole body with the same wave, they are confirming: "Yes, I am in hypnosis." Each subsequent muscle is another brick in the foundation of trance.

📜 Full Script — Eyelid Catalepsy + Body Scan

This text can be read aloud. Adapt the pace and intonation to the client's state. Pauses are marked with …

— Beginning of Induction —

"Close your eyes… Take a deep breath… and as you exhale, allow your whole body to relax…

Now focus your attention on your eyelids… Just feel them… And begin to relax them deeper and deeper… As deeply as possible…

Relax your eyelids as deeply as possible — so deeply that they will not open…

Good… Now, maintaining that deep relaxation in your eyelids, test them and make sure they won't open… Move your eyebrows, wrinkle your forehead… Feel how your eyelids stay relaxed and closed…

Excellent… If they open — just close them again and continue relaxing even deeper…

Now take that feeling of relaxation from your eyelids and fill your whole body with it… Send it like a wave — from the top of your head to the tips of your fingers and toes…

Fill every cell… every muscle… Let it flow downward — through your face… neck… shoulders… arms… chest… stomach… back… hips… legs… and all the way down to your feet…

Every muscle in your body is now relaxed… You can feel warmth spreading through your whole body… Relaxation deepens with every exhale…

And in this deep relaxation, you can hear my voice… and only my voice… Everything else fades into the background…

Excellent… Well done… Just stay in this state…"

— Next: Transition to deepening (stairs / count) —

💡 This script is the basic structure. Over time, you'll learn to vary the wording, add your own phrases, and adapt to the client. The key is maintaining the sequence: eyelid catalepsy → test → relaxation wave.

🪑 Operator's Body Position and Feedback

While you work with the client, your own posture and behavior matter. Elman paid special attention to this.

📌 Hand on the Shoulder

Keep one hand on the client's shoulder. This is not just a touch — it's a feedback channel. Through your hand you can feel:

  • Body warmth — when the client enters trance, the shoulder begins to warm up. This is a sign of vasodilation and increased blood flow — an objective marker of trance
  • Muscle tension — if the shoulder remains rigid, the client hasn't fully relaxed
  • Micro-movements — slight twitching indicates the body is reorganizing, releasing tension

Touch also works as an anchor: over time, just touching the shoulder will be enough for the client to instantly return to a trance state.

🗣️ Verbal Feedback

During the induction, use approving words: "excellent", "well done", "good". They serve two functions:

  • Reinforcement — the client knows they are doing everything right. This removes the anxiety of "I'm not doing it right"
  • Rhythm — these short words between instructions create a natural speech rhythm that holds the client's attention

Don't overdo it. One "excellent" after each completed step is enough. Too much praise starts to sound like empty chatter and distracts from the trance.

🎙️ Voice Congruence

Your voice should be relaxed, but not monotonous. If you say "relaxation," your voice sounds relaxed — slightly slower, slightly lower. If you say "deeper," your voice goes down, becoming more resonant. This is called congruence — matching intonation to content.

A monotonous voice puts people to sleep, rather than inducing trance. The difference is that when falling asleep, a person simply loses consciousness, but in trance, they remain suggestible and focused on your voice. Congruent intonations hold that attention.

🧠 Exercise: Read any phrase from the script aloud, changing your intonation on each key word. "Relaxation" — voice lower. "Deeper" — even lower. "Wave" — smooth rise and fall. Consciously practice this variation.

⏱️ The Importance of Pace — No Pauses Between Steps

Between the eyelid relaxation and sending the relaxation wave, there should be no long pauses. Once you have confirmation (eyelids won't open), immediately move to the body scan. Why this matters:

  • Trance dynamics — if you pause, the client may "surface" from the initial state and start analyzing what's happening. Trance is built on continuity
  • Using momentum — the first phenomenon (eyelid catalepsy) creates an impulse. You must use that impulse immediately, before it fades
  • Don't let the conscious mind engage — between suggestions, the conscious mind may start asking questions: "What's happening?", "Why am I doing this?", "Is it working?". A smooth, continuous flow of speech leaves no room for these questions

In practice, it looks like this: you say "excellent, your eyelids stay closed" and immediately, without a pause, continue with "now take that feeling and fill your whole body with it." One phrase flows smoothly into the next.

🧠 The Golden Mean: Your speech speed should be relaxed, but without hanging pauses. Imagine you're leading a client by the hand through a dark corridor — you move calmly but confidently, without stopping. If you stop, they'll start looking around.

📝 Practical Assignment

Goal: Practice eyelid catalepsy and body scan on 3 different people. Achieve a state where the client cannot open their eyes at your request.

  1. Seat your partner, check body position (feet on the floor, hands on knees). Obtain the three permissions from Lesson 1.1.
  2. Deliver the hypnotic contract (Lesson 1.1) and check compliance.
  3. Give the eyelid relaxation suggestion: "Relax your eyelids so deeply that they won't open."
  4. Test: "Maintaining the relaxation, check that they won't open. Move your eyebrows, wrinkle your forehead."
  5. If the client opened their eyes — use the return phrase: "You saw they open. Relax them to the point where they won't open."
  6. Once the eyelids are fixed — immediately, without pause, send the relaxation wave through the body (body scan).
  7. Keep your hand on the client's shoulder throughout the process. Note when the shoulder starts to warm up.

Success criterion: The client keeps their eyes closed after the test and reports feeling warmth and relaxation throughout their body.

🧠 Practice journal: After each session, record: how long the eyelid catalepsy took (in seconds); what reaction you got on the test (opened eyes / didn't open); whether you felt shoulder warmth; what words the client used to describe their state after the body scan. Compare results across 3 different people.

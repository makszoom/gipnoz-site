1.5 — Tests: Eyelid Catalepsy and Analgesia

Module 1 • ~20 minutes

🎬 Video will be inserted after upload to YouTube

Why Tests Are Needed

After numbers have disappeared (Lesson 1.4), you know the person has crossed the somnambulism threshold. But the person doesn't know that. They may not realize they were in hypnosis — especially on the first session.

Tests serve two purposes:

  • Demonstration to the client: show the person that hypnosis really works. When they can't open their eyes, even though they genuinely try — that convinces more than any words.
  • Confirmation for the operator: you get objective proof that the person is in deep trance, not just "sitting quietly with their eyes closed."

The eyelid catalepsy test is the simplest and most visual. It's hard to fake, easy to observe, and it makes a strong impression on the client.

🧠 Key idea: Eyelid catalepsy is a light-trance test that you give to a person already in deep trance (somnambulism). It MUST work because the subconscious has accepted the suggestion and the critical conscious mind is offline.

Eyelid Catalepsy: The Paradoxical Suggestion

When numbers have disappeared, you confirm: "Are they all gone? Good. You are in deep hypnosis." Now you can move on to the test.

The key technique is a paradoxical suggestion: you give a command that contradicts everyday logic. The more the person tries to break it, the stronger it takes hold.

The core phrase: "The harder you try to open them, the tighter they squeeze shut."

The conscious mind receives a contradiction: "open" vs "squeeze even tighter." The subconscious chooses the stronger suggestion — tightening. With every effort to open, the eyelids close tighter. This turns the test into a demonstration — the person sees for themselves that their body is obeying the subconscious.

Full Eyelid Catalepsy Script

This script is used AFTER the somnambulism threshold has been crossed (numbers disappeared). Read slowly, with pauses.

"Notice the contact of your closed eyelids… they begin to flutter slightly and squeeze together…"

"I'm going to count from 5 down to 1, and with each number they will squeeze tighter… and tighter…"

[touch the forehead with a finger]

"5… they are squeezing… 4… squeezing tighter… 3… as if glued together with superglue… 2… eyelids are sealed shut… 1… your eyes are completely closed and you cannot open them…"

"Want this to happen… allow it to happen…"

"Now try to open them…" (quickly and confidently) "…but they only squeeze tighter… the more you try, the tighter they stay closed…"

"When you're sure you cannot open them — say: I am convinced"

[wait for confirmation "I am convinced" or a nod]

"Good. Stop trying. Just let them stay closed… and relax even deeper…"

⚠️ Important: The command "Try to open them" must be delivered quickly and confidently, without pause. If you pause — the conscious mind may seize control. You must get ahead of their attempt with your suggestion.

Why Eyelid Catalepsy Works in Deep Trance

Eyelid catalepsy after number amnesia works for three reasons:

  1. Somnambulism: the critical factor of the conscious mind is disabled. The person doesn't evaluate the suggestion — they accept it.
  2. Paradox: "the harder you try to open, the tighter they squeeze" creates a situation where conscious effort reinforces the subconscious suggestion.
  3. Tactile reinforcement: touching the forehead (or bridge of the nose) during the count from 5 to 1 anchors the suggestion to a physical sensation.

🧠 Visual signal: When the person tries to open their eyes, you see the eyelids tense, but the eye slit does not open — lashes flutter, eyelids vibrate, but remain sealed. That is catalepsy. If the eye slit opens even a tiny bit — the suggestion didn't fully take.

Analgesia — The Pain Suppression Test

After successful eyelid catalepsy, you can perform an additional check — analgesia (pain suppression). This is a more subtle test that shows how deeply the subconscious has taken control of the body.

Technique:

"In this state, nothing I do will cause you the slightest discomfort… You will feel nothing but a touch…"

"Your subconscious completely controls all sensations in the body… And it can switch off any signals of discomfort…"

[take the skin on the wrist (back side) between your thumb and index finger and squeeze — not with fingernails! — but with the pads of your fingers, with noticeable but not injuring pressure]

"You feel nothing but a light touch… Just soft pressure…"

"When I remove my hand — that touch will disappear, and you will relax even deeper…"

How to interpret the reaction:

  • ✅ No reaction: the person's expression doesn't change, breathing is even — analgesia accepted, suggestion working
  • ⚠️ Micro-reaction: slight change in expression, micro-movement of the eyebrow — suggestion partially accepted, can be reinforced
  • ❌ Pain: the person pulls their hand away, winces, groans — suggestion not accepted. The subconscious retains control over pain sensations

⚠️ Important: If the person feels pain — don't insist and don't repeat. Just note that the trance depth is insufficient. Go back to deepening or use another session. Analgesia is an advanced test; it may not work on the first try.

Analgesia as a Therapeutic Skill

The ability to induce analgesia is not just a test. It's an important therapeutic tool. In more advanced sessions, you'll be able to:

  • Relieve chronic pain (headaches, muscle pain, joint pain)
  • Work with discomfort during medical procedures
  • Create resource anchors for a "pain-free state"

But at this stage, analgesia is a diagnostic test. It shows how deeply the subconscious has taken control of the body. If analgesia didn't work — don't be discouraged. It means the person needs more time and more practice.

Coming Out of Trance

After the tests, you need to properly bring the person out of trance. This is just as important as the induction itself.

The correct formula:

"Now I'm going to count from 1 to 3… on the count of 3, but not before, open your eyes… and notice how good you feel…"

"1… begin to return… become aware of your body…"

"2… feel the room, the sounds around you…"

"3 — open your eyes… take a deep breath… how do you feel?"

Critically important:

  • Never say "wake up" — these are medical terms that create unwanted associations
  • The phrase "but not before" gives the subconscious time for a smooth emergence — the person is not "yanked" out of trance
  • "Notice how good you feel" — this is a post-hypnotic suggestion that reinforces a positive attitude toward the session

The 30–90 Second Post-Hypnotic Window

After coming out of trance, the person retains heightened suggestibility for approximately 30–90 seconds. This is the "post-hypnotic window" — a moment when the subconscious is still open and the conscious mind is only just returning.

Use this window for:

  • Positive suggestions: "And each time it will become easier and easier for you to enter trance"
  • Reinforcing the experience: "Notice how pleasant the feeling of deep relaxation is"
  • Anchoring: "The very word 'hypnosis' will bring you back to this pleasant state"

Don't clutter this window with complex instructions. A simple, short, positive suggestion is ideal.

📝 Practical Assignment

Goal: Perform eyelid catalepsy and the analgesia test on a partner in somnambulism.

  1. Bring your partner into somnambulism through the full protocol (1.1–1.4).
  2. After numbers disappear, perform eyelid catalepsy using the script from Lesson 1.5.
  3. Record: did the partner say "I am convinced"? What was the reaction when trying to open the eyes?
  4. Perform the analgesia test — squeeze the skin on the wrist without fingernails.
  5. Record: was there a pain reaction? If so, what kind?
  6. Properly bring the partner out of trance with the 1–3 count. Use the post-hypnotic window (one short phrase).
  7. Ask: "What did you feel when you tried to open your eyes but couldn't?"
  8. Repeat with 2 different partners. Compare whose catalepsy worked faster.

🧠 Tip: For many people, the first eyelid catalepsy is a shock in the best sense. They are genuinely surprised they can't open their eyes. Don't break that surprise with unnecessary comments. Just say: "This is how hypnosis works. Your subconscious is stronger than you thought."

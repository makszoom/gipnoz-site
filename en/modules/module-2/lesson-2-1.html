2.1 — Stabilizing Somnambulism

Module 2 • ~20 minutes

🎬 Video will be embedded after upload to YouTube

🎚️ The Analog Nature of Hypnosis

Hypnosis is not a light switch. A person does not toggle between "in trance" and "not in trance" like an on/off bulb. Trance depth oscillates — it fluctuates in waves.

After you've successfully performed an Elman induction and the person has crossed the somnambulism threshold, their state does not lock in at that depth automatically. It can "float up" — consciousness tries to regain control, and depth decreases. This is normal. The hypnotist's job is to stabilize the state below the somnambulism threshold.

Think of it like scuba diving: you've descended to depth, but to stay there you need to compensate for pressure and make micro-adjustments. Without stabilization, you'll simply be pushed back to the surface.

🧠 Key principle: Stabilization is not a one-time action — it's a continuous process. You haven't "reached" depth and stopped. You are constantly working to keep the person below threshold. Every word you say either deepens or allows them to float up.

🔟 Countdown from 10 to 1 with Shoulder Press

The primary stabilization tool after induction is a reverse count from 10 to 1, synchronized with breathing and reinforced by a tactile anchor.

How to perform:

  • Sync with breathing: each number is spoken on the exhale. At the peak of the inhale — a brief press on the client's shoulder.
  • Each number = deeper: each subsequent number is accompanied by the suggestion that trance is getting deeper. "10 — deeper… 9 — even deeper… 8 — twice as deep…"
  • Shoulder press: creates a non-verbal suggestion. A tactile anchor that the subconscious links to "sinking down," deepening.
  • Tied to natural rhythm: "Every exhale takes you deeper" — use a natural physiological process to guide the person deeper without resistance.

Complete 10→1 Script

"Now I'm going to count from 10 down to 1… and with every number, with every exhale, you will sink deeper and deeper…"

[brief pause, wait for the client's natural inhale]

"10 — deep… [press shoulder at peak of inhale] and with the exhale — even deeper…"

"9 — deeper… [press shoulder] with every exhale, deeper and deeper…"

"8 — twice as deep… [press]…"

"7 — deeper and deeper… [press]…"

"6 — deeper still… [press]…"

"5 — halfway there… [press] and with every exhale — deeper…"

"4 — deep inside… [press]…"

"3 — very deep… [press]…"

"2 — almost at the deepest level… [press]…"

"1 — full immersion… [press] you are at the very deepest depth…"

[Pause 3-5 seconds, let the person "settle" at that depth]

"And now, at this depth, you can hear me clearly… and every word I speak sinks you even deeper…"

🧠 Technique: Don't rush. Pause 3-5 seconds between numbers to let the subconscious "digest" each suggestion. Watch the client's breathing — if they're holding their breath, adjust your pace. The exhale should be natural, without effort.

📣 The Four Stabilization Phrases

In the Kayfabe method, four key phrases form the stabilization framework — the minimum set of suggestions that keep a person in somnambulism. These phrases should be delivered after the 10→1 count, at the very start of working at depth.

You can repeat them in different combinations throughout the session whenever you notice depth starting to decrease.

Phrase 1 — Isolation from Noise:

"Any sounds around you do not bother you at all, they only help you relax deeper."

→ This phrase turns any external noise (a door closing, a car outside, voices) from a distraction into an aid. The subconscious stops filtering sounds as a threat.

Phrase 2 — Anchoring the Voice:

"The sound of my voice takes you deeper."

→ Ties deepening to the very act of speaking. Now every word you speak automatically deepens the trance. This creates a self-sustaining cycle.

Phrase 3 — Linking Depth and Well-Being:

"The deeper you go, the better you feel."

→ Connects the concept of depth with pleasant sensations. The subconscious starts to seek depth because it associates it with comfort.

Phrase 4 — The Positive Loop:

"And the better you feel, the deeper you go."

→ Completes the cycle. Now it's "depth → good feeling → even deeper." This is positive feedback that works automatically, without your involvement.

🧠 How to use: Don't read all four phrases one after another like a checklist. Weave them into natural speech. For example: "You feel how the sound of my voice takes you deeper… and the deeper you go, the better you feel… and the better you feel, the deeper you go… and any sounds around you only help…"

🚫 Why NOT to Use Stair and Elevator Metaphors

Many beginner hypnotists use imagery metaphors for deepening: "imagine you're walking down stairs," "you're riding an elevator down," "each step takes you deeper." This approach is strongly discouraged for three reasons:

1. Phobias and Triggers

The person may have claustrophobia (fear of enclosed spaces) or a fear of heights. An elevator for such a person is a source of anxiety, not relaxation. Stairs can be associated with a fall injury. You don't know what associations your image will trigger, so don't risk it.

2. Unnecessary Cognitive Load

When you ask a person to "imagine" stairs, you load their conscious mind with visualization. Instead of going deeper, they spend mental resources creating an image. In somnambulism, the conscious mind must be maximally passive, not busy visualizing.

3. Distracts from the Hypnotist's Voice

Visualization pulls the client's attention away from your voice. They "go into the picture" and stop responding to your suggestions. Your voice is the main deepening tool. No metaphor can replace a direct suggestion: "you are sinking deeper."

🧠 Rule: The more direct the suggestion, the more effective it is. Counting from 10 to 1 works better than any metaphor because it requires nothing from the subconscious except reacting to a simple, intuitively understood decreasing number. 10 → 1 = deep. That's enough.

🔄 The Positive Loop: Depth → Good → Deeper

The positive loop is a self-sustaining cycle that makes deepening automatic. Once you've established the loop, you don't need to repeat suggestions — it works on its own.

The deeper you relax
↓
the better you feel
↓
the better you feel
↓
the deeper you go

This loop uses a natural psychological reaction: any state that feels pleasant, the subconscious seeks to amplify. You connect "depth" — a concept that can be frightening for a beginner — with "well-being," which the subconscious always wants to maximize.

As a result, trance depth becomes self-reinforcing. The person doesn't just "stay" in trance on your command — their own subconscious works to deepen it because it finds it pleasant.

🧠 Key point: The loop only works if you deliver it in the right order and give the subconscious time to "absorb" each part. Don't rush. After phrase 3, pause 3-5 seconds before moving to phrase 4.

📝 Practice Assignment

Goal: Perform a complete 10→1 deepening on a partner with stabilization phrases.

  1. Induce trance in your partner using the Elman protocol (Module 1).
  2. Immediately after eye closure and eyelid lock, begin counting from 10 to 1 with a shoulder press at the peak of each inhale.
  3. After the count, deliver the four stabilization phrases, weaving them into natural speech.
  4. Be sure to state the positive loop: "the deeper → the better → the better → the deeper."
  5. Test depth: ask them to lift their arm — in somnambulism the arm will rise smoothly and stay in the air.
  6. End the session with a gentle emergence. Ask your partner: "On a scale of 1 to 10, how deep did you feel?"

🧠 Note: Some partners may "float up" after the first stabilization phrases — go back to counting, but from 5 to 1 instead of 10. That's usually enough to restore depth. The more you practice, the fewer stabilization cycles you'll need.

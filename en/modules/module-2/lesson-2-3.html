2.3 — Working with Resistance (The Talk)

Module 2 • ~20 minutes

🎬 Video will be embedded after upload to YouTube

You Are the Only One Running the Session

In hypnosis, there is only one captain of the ship — the hypnotist. If the client takes over control, the session goes down the drain. Even if they do it unintentionally, out of the best intentions or simply "helping" — it still destroys hypnosis.

Every time a client interferes with the process, they step out of being a passive observer and become an active participant. And hypnosis only works when the client allows themselves to be led, rather than leading.

🧠 Key rule: If the client starts directing the process — you're no longer hypnotizing. You're just talking.

Signs of Resistance

Resistance doesn't always look like "I don't want to." It often masquerades as trying too hard or misunderstanding. Here are the main signs:

  • Doesn't follow instructions — closes eyes before you say so; helps lift the arm instead of letting it rise; opens eyes before the command.
  • Doesn't let go of numbers — continues counting after you've finished the reverse count; adds their own numbers; counts faster or slower than your rhythm.
  • Opens eyes during eyelid catalepsy — you checked, eyelids are locked, and the person opens their eyes when you look away or even while you're watching.
  • Analyzes instead of following — comments on the process, asks questions during trance, says "what was that?" in the middle of the session.

🧠 Important: Some clients do this because they are trying to help. They think hypnosis is when they actively participate. Your job is to explain that the best help is simply to allow.

The Talk — When the Client Doesn't Listen

"The Talk" is a firm but respectful procedure for taking back control. It's used when a client consciously fails to follow instructions. The script works like a reset — you stop the session, look the client in the eyes, and return yourself to the role of leader.

Full The Talk Script

"Open your eyes. Look at me."

[Pause. Establish eye contact. Speak calmly, firmly.]

"I can help you. But for that, you need to follow my instructions."

"Think about everything you've tried before and how well it worked. It didn't. Otherwise we wouldn't be meeting today."

"I believe I can help you. I know how this works. But for that, you need to follow my instructions."

"Decide."

The silence rule: After you say "Decide" — stay silent. Whoever speaks first loses. Like in sales. The client must make a decision. Give them the silence.

🧠 Key principle: The Talk is not punishment or aggression. It's a return to structure. You say this not because you're angry, but because you're a professional and you know that without structure, hypnosis doesn't work.

When to Use The Talk and When to Just Correct

Not every resistance requires The Talk. It's important to distinguish:

  • The person just didn't understand — rephrase the instruction. Say it differently, use other words. "Close your eyes when I count to three, not before" instead of just "close your eyes."
  • The person consciously doesn't follow — does it their own way, ignores your words, comments and analyzes — use The Talk.
  • The person is nervous or afraid — not The Talk, but additional pre-talk. Remove the fear, explain safety.
  • The person is "helping" — gentle correction: "You don't need to help. Just let it happen. The best help is to relax and listen to my voice."

Case Study: A Woman with Trauma

In the transcript, the master describes a case with a female client who couldn't work with a male hypnotist due to psychological trauma. She resisted not because she didn't want to change, but because a trigger fired on a male voice, on male presence.

In this situation, The Talk wouldn't have worked — the problem wasn't a lack of structure, but a psychological defense. The master reframed the approach: used impersonal suggestions, worked through metaphors, gave the client an illusion of control so she could relax.

Takeaway: The Talk is for fighting resistance. Metaphors and reframing are for working with trauma. Learn to distinguish these situations.

🧠 Lesson: If resistance is caused by fear or trauma — don't push. Provide safety. If resistance is caused by a desire to control — The Talk puts you back in the driver's seat.

My Way or Highway — Short Version

If you don't want to go into the full The Talk, use the short version mid-session, without coming out of trance:

"You're here so I can help you. And for that, you need to do what I say. If you want to do it your way — you've already tried that. And it didn't work. So now — it's my turn."

"Just relax and listen to my voice. I'll take care of everything else."

📝 Practice Assignment

Goal: Practice The Talk in a "resistant client" role-play.

  1. Find a partner for practice.
  2. The partner plays the role of a "resistant client" — closes eyes before the command, helps lift the arm, analyzes out loud.
  3. You apply The Talk: stop the session, look them in the eyes, say the script, stay silent.
  4. The partner should feel the difference between "I'm trying" and "I'm allowing."
  5. Switch roles.

🧠 Advice: When practicing The Talk, pay attention to your tone. It should be calm, firm, confident — not angry, not irritated. You're a professional simply stating the facts.

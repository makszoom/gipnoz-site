2.4 — Direct Suggestions: Basic Protocol

Module 2 • ~25 minutes

🎬 Video will be embedded after upload to YouTube

Three Questions Before the Session

Before you give suggestions, you need to know exactly what you're suggesting. You can't work with "abstract improvement." You need the client's own specific words.

  1. "What do you want?" — only positive wording. Not "I don't want to be anxious," but "I want to feel calm." Ban the word "not." If the client says "I don't want" — rephrase: "And what do you want instead?"
  2. "How will you know you have it?" — the criterion. What will change in their life? How will they feel? What will others notice? This gives you a measurable goal — and an image for the suggestion.
  3. "Why do you want this?" — motivation. Why is this important? What will it give them? Without deep motivation, suggestions won't work — the subconscious sees no reason to change.

🧠 Key rule: Use the client's words. If they said "confidence" — use "confidence." If they said "ease" — use "ease." Scripts from the internet with someone else's words don't work because they don't resonate with that specific person's subconscious.

Four Language Constructions for Suggestions

These are the basic linguistic techniques that make your suggestions effective. Master them — and you'll be able to build a suggestion for any request in 30 seconds.

1. Presupposition of Awareness

You presuppose that what you're talking about is already happening. The subconscious accepts it as fact.

"You will notice how calmness spreads through your body…"

"You will see how much easier it has become to breathe…"

"You will realize that confidence is already here…"

2. Presupposition of Degree

You don't ask "will it" or "won't it" — you ask "how much."

"Notice how much more confident you feel…"

"Notice how much easier it is to feel calm about this now…"

"Notice how much deeper your trance has become…"

3. The "The… The…" Link

Creates a cause-and-effect relationship where one thing amplifies another. A very powerful construction.

"The deeper you go into trance, the better you feel…"

"The more you relax, the more confident your inner voice becomes…"

"The calmer your body, the clearer your mind…"

4. "Since / As If"

Provides logical justification or creates a metaphor for deepening.

"Since you've allowed yourself to go into hypnosis, your subconscious is open to positive changes…"

"As if twice as deep…"

"As if every breath brings new confidence…"

Law of Compounding

Each subsequent suggestion amplifies the previous one. Suggestions don't work in isolation — they work like a chain reaction.

Example: if you first say "you feel calm," then "you notice how much deeper this calm is than a minute ago" — the second suggestion doesn't cancel the first, it layers on top of it. The more suggestions, the stronger the effect.

How to use: Build suggestions so that each subsequent one references the previous. "You've already noticed the calm… And now notice how this calm becomes deeper… And the deeper it becomes, the more confident you feel…"

🧠 Golden rule: One suggestion — good. Five linked suggestions — breakthrough. Ten — transformation.

Positive Images, Not Negative

The subconscious does not understand negations. If you say "don't be anxious" — the subconscious hears "be anxious" and does exactly that. It moves toward images, not away from them.

  • ❌ "You will not be afraid" → subconscious creates fear
  • ✅ "You feel calm and confident" → subconscious creates calmness
  • ❌ "Stop being nervous" → amplifies nervousness
  • ✅ "You are relaxing deeper and deeper" → amplifies relaxation

Always formulate a suggestion as something that appears, not something that disappears.

Example: Building a Suggestion for a Specific Request

Client's request: "I want to feel confident in job interviews. I'll know it's working when I stop stammering and can answer questions clearly. I need this to get the job I want."

Client's words: confidence, interviews, answer clearly, get the job.

The Constructed Suggestion

"And now, being in this deep, calm state, you begin to notice… how a new confidence arises inside you…" [presupposition of awareness]

"The more deeply relaxed you are, the more confident your inner voice becomes…" [the… the…]

"Notice how much calmer and clearer your speech becomes…" [presupposition of degree + client's words]

"Since your subconscious has already chosen confidence, every word you speak sounds firm and clear…" [since + outcome]

"As if you're already in that interview — and you feel completely calm, answering clearly and confidently…" [as if + scenario]

"And with every word, with every breath, this confidence grows stronger… deeper… more natural…" [compounding]

📝 Practice Assignment

Goal: Compose a suggestion for a fictional request using all four language constructions and the law of compounding.

Request: "I want to sleep peacefully through the night. I'll know it's working when I stop waking up at 3 AM with anxiety. I need this to restore my energy and be productive at work."

  1. Extract the client's key words: peacefully, sleep, night, not waking up, anxiety → reframe the negative into positive ("deep sleep," "calmness," "restoration").
  2. Compose a 5-7 sentence suggestion using all four constructions.
  3. Read the finished suggestion to a partner (in trance or just aloud — evaluate how it sounds).
  4. Write down your version in your notes — it will be useful for a real session.

🧠 Advice: Don't try to memorize the constructions mechanically. Practice building suggestions on the fly — first with notes, then without. After 10 practices, it will become automatic.

🧍 Using Balance to Enter Trance

Standing induction is one of the most impressive and simultaneously most accessible methods of instant hypnosis. It uses the body's natural physiological reaction to losing balance to create a spike and enter trance.

This method is especially valuable because it engages the whole body — not just a hand or voice, but the entire balance system. When the body loses stability, the brain instantly shifts attention from the external world to the internal sense of equilibrium — and this creates an ideal window for suggestion.

The technique consists of the sequence: suggestion of stability → rocking → loss of balance → "sleep" command → catching and deepening.

🧠 Important: Safety is priority number one. You must always be ready to catch the person so they don't fall. Never work standing with people who have vestibular issues or on slippery floors.

🎯 Step 1 — Suggestion of Stability and Creating Focus

Ask the client to stand up straight, feet shoulder-width apart, arms loosely at their sides. Eyes closed or open — depending on the version of the technique.

Suggesting Immobility

You begin by suggesting that the person stands stable — paradoxically, this creates a slight tension in the body, which can then be "released" to create shock:

"Stand straight. Feet shoulder-width apart. Feel your feet on the floor. You're standing stable. Very stable. Your body is like a rock. Nothing can move you from this spot…"

Creating Focus on a Point of Contact

The next step is to create a point of focus. You place your hand on the client's forehead (or shoulder, or just above the chest) and draw attention to this touch:

"Now I'm placing my hand on your forehead. Feel my palm. All your attention — only on this touch. You feel the warmth of my hand. The pressure. You're stable, but all your attention is on my hand…"

🧠 Why this works: When you suggest stability, the subconscious checks this assertion — is the body really stable? This micro-process already engages the subconscious. And the touch to the head creates a focus point that "anchors" attention to your hand.

🌀 Step 2 — Rocking the Head and Body

After attention is focused, you begin to gently rock the client's head (or body). This creates a feeling of losing control over the body — an important element that prepares the ground for shock.

Rocking the Head

Hand on the forehead, you begin very slowly and gently to tilt the client's head back-and-forth or side-to-side. The amplitude is small — no more than 5–10 degrees. Importantly: you are not pushing, but leading the movement, creating the illusion that the head is starting to move on its own:

"Your head is becoming light… very light… it's starting to sway gently… forward… backward… [movement] easily and freely… by itself…"

Rocking the Body

A deeper version — rocking the entire body. For this, the hand is placed on the chest or shoulder, and you rock the person forward and backward, gradually increasing the amplitude:

"You feel your body starting to sway… like a branch in the wind… forward… backward… stronger now… [amplitude grows]… you can't stop… your body keeps moving on its own…"

Psychological Effect

When a person feels their body moving "on its own," without their conscious control, it creates a powerful suggestion: "I don't control my body." The critical factor weakens because the person experiences direct evidence that contradicts their usual sense of control. This is ideal preparation for an instant induction.

🧠 Technique: Don't rush. The rocking phase should last 15–30 seconds, until you feel that the client's body has really "floated" and the movement has become smooth, almost automatic. The deeper this phase, the stronger the effect of the subsequent shock.

⚡ Step 3 — Loss of Balance and the "Sleep" Command

At the moment when the client's body is most relaxed and the movement has become automatic, you create shock — by suddenly removing their point of support.

Option A — Remove the Hand from the Forehead

At the moment when the head is tilted back and your hand is supporting it, you sharply remove your hand. The client's head (and body) loses its support and begins to fall backward. Simultaneously — the command "SLEEP!"

Option B — A Light Push

Instead of removing your hand, you give a light but unexpected push to the chest or shoulder. The person loses their balance for a second. This option requires more skill to avoid frightening or injuring the client.

Critical Timing

As with the hand induction, the "sleep" command must sound simultaneously with the loss of balance or within the first 0.25 seconds. The brain is busy processing the unexpected change in body position — and the command goes directly to the subconscious.

"…your head is tilting back… further and further… [hand is sharply removed] — SLEEP!"

🧠 Safety: When using Option A (removing the hand), you must be ready to CATCH the person with your other hand on their back. The person should not fall to the floor — they should gently lower onto your supporting arm. This simultaneously creates safety and reinforces trust.

🤲 Step 4 — Catching and Instant Deepening

The most impressive part — and the most responsible one. At the moment the person loses balance and you give the "sleep" command, your other hand should already be ready to catch them by the back.

The Catch

You gently but confidently take the weight of the client's body onto your arm. The person ends up in a semi-reclined position on your arm — like a dancer in a dip. This creates a powerful feeling of safety and trust.

Deepening While Held

Without removing your hand, start speaking — right in this position. Use the very fact that the person is "hanging" on your arm as a suggestion:

"…SLEEP! Deep. Completely. You're safe. I've got you. Relax. Feel my arm holding you? You're completely safe. With every word — deeper. Relaxation flows from your head to your toes. You're at the deepest level…"

Transition to a Seated Position

After 15–20 seconds, when you're sure the person is in trance, gently sit them down on a chair (which should be prepared in advance behind them). Keep talking during this movement:

"I'm going to help you sit down now… and even as you sit, you continue to sink deeper… [seating them] there you go… deeper and deeper…"

🧠 Key Point: The entire process — from the start of rocking to sitting on the chair — must be smooth and continuous. Any pause, any "hesitation" breaks the trance. You are the conductor; every action leads to the next, without stopping.

📜 Full Script for Standing Induction

Step 1 — Setup and Focus:

"Stand straight, feet shoulder-width apart. Arms loose. Close your eyes. Feel your feet on the floor. You're standing stable. Very stable. [Places hand on forehead] Feel my palm on your forehead? Warmth. Pressure. All attention — only on this."

Step 2 — Rocking:

"Your head is becoming light… very light… it's starting to sway… by itself… forward… [gentle movement] backward… [movement]… easily and freely… with every movement you relax more and more…"

Step 3 — Loss of Balance + Command:

"…head is tilting back… further and further… and when I say 'sleep' — you will completely relax… further back… [removes hand] — SLEEP!"

Step 4 — Catch + Deepening:

"[Catches by the back] I've got you. You're safe. Complete relaxation. You hear my voice — with every word, deeper. Relax even more. Your whole body is soft, heavy, relaxed. Good. Very deep."

Step 5 — Seating:

"Now I'm going to help you sit down. Keep relaxing. [Seats them] There you go. Deeper. With every exhale, deeper. Complete peace."

🛡️ Safety and Contraindications

Standing induction with loss of balance is a physically active technique. It requires special attention to safety:

  • Always have a spotter: Your other hand must be ready to catch the body. Never perform this induction if you're not physically prepared to support the person.
  • Check the space: There should be no sharp furniture corners, slippery surfaces, or obstacles nearby.
  • Prepare a chair: Place a sturdy chair behind the client at a step's distance, so you can seat them after they enter trance.
  • Do not work with: People with vestibular issues, dizziness, epilepsy, late-term pregnancy, or elderly with fragile bones.
  • Explain beforehand: Tell the client: "I will hold you; you won't fall. Trust me."

🧠 Rule: Standing induction is a controlled loss of balance, not a fall. The person should not get hurt or be frightened. If you're not confident in your catch — practice with a mattress or on a soft surface until you feel confident.

🚫 Typical Mistakes

❌ Transition to Shock Too Fast

If you didn't give the body enough time for rocking and relaxation, the shock will be "cold" — the nervous system hasn't entered automatic movement mode, and the spike won't work. Spend at least 15–20 seconds on rocking.

❌ Unconfident Catch

If you catch the person hesitantly or too late, they'll feel the fear of falling — and the trance will break. Your supporting hand must already be moving at the moment you say "sleep."

❌ Forgot the Chair

After entering trance, you need somewhere to sit the person down. If there's no chair, you're stuck in an awkward position, lose concentration — and the trance breaks. Prepare a chair in advance.

🧠 Tip: If you're doing this induction for the first time, start with a tiny amplitude — literally 2–3 centimeters of deviation. Gradually increase the amplitude as you gain confidence. Better a "partial trance" than an injury.

📝 Practice Assignment

Goal: Perform a complete standing induction with a catch and seating.

  1. Prepare the space: a sturdy chair behind, clear area around.
  2. Explain the process to your partner: "I will hold you; you won't fall."
  3. Execute all 5 steps: focus → rocking → loss of balance → command → catch → deepening → seating.
  4. After seating, check depth: eyelid catalepsy, arm levitation.
  5. End the session with a gentle exit.
  6. Ask your partner: "At what moment did you feel the greatest loss of control? Was it scary or pleasant?"

🧠 Advanced version: Try the same induction without prior rocking — only through the sharp removal of the hand from the forehead. It's harder but faster. Compare the results of both versions.

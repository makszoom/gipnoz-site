3.6 — When to Use: Context and Safety

Module 3 • 15 min

📅 Choosing the Technique by Session Number

One of the most common questions is: "Can I use a rapid induction on a new client right away?" The answer depends on context, but there is a general rule based on Jerry Kain's experience.

First Session — Only Full Elman Induction

On the first meeting, the client needs to experience hypnosis in its full form. A complete Elman induction (6–10 steps) gives the client an understanding of the process: what hypnosis is, how trance feels, what is expected of them. If you "shock" a person into trance on the first session, they may:

  • Become frightened by the loss of control
  • Not understand what happened ("was that hypnosis? I was just confused")
  • Lose trust — "something was done to me without my knowledge"

Exception: if the client already has experience with hypnosis from other practitioners and specifically asks to "go deep immediately."

Second Session — Shortened Elman or Rapid Induction

After trust is established and the client knows what trance is, you can speed things up. Use a shortened version of Elman (3–5 steps) or one of the rapid inductions from this module. The client already knows what to expect, and their subconscious responds faster to familiar triggers.

Third+ Session — Rapid or Instant Induction

Starting from the third session, when the client fully trusts you and their subconscious is already trained to enter trance, you can use instant techniques (shock, verbal shock, fist method). The client enters trance in seconds — and this is perceived as a mark of skill.

🏥 Therapeutic Context vs. Demonstration

Context strongly influences technique choice.

In Therapy

  • Speed is not the goal. Results matter more than impressions. The client may need time to "feel" hypnosis.
  • Rapid induction may not give the feeling of hypnosis. The client might think: "I was just disoriented, that wasn't hypnosis. Nothing happened to me."
  • Recommendation: in early sessions, use full or shortened inductions. Add instant techniques when the client already enters trance confidently.

In Demonstrations

  • Speed and showmanship matter. Instant inductions impress audiences and confirm the hypnotist's skill.
  • Impress away. Rapid trance entry is the "calling card" of stage hypnosis.
  • For demos, choose suggestible subjects (Highs — highly hypnotizable), pre-tested beforehand.

🛡️ Safety Techniques

Instant and rapid inductions are powerful tools, and if used incorrectly, they can cause harm. Be sure to follow these rules:

  • Always suggest balance when working standing. If you induce trance in a standing position, the subconscious may interpret this as "loss of consciousness" — and the person may fall. Always give the suggestion: "You maintain your balance, but your body is completely relaxed."
  • Be ready to catch. Even with a balance suggestion, some subjects may start to topple. Always keep your hands ready to catch the head and shoulders.
  • Check for neck, back, and arm injuries. Before shock inductions with jerks (handshake, drop), always ask: "Do you have any neck, back, or arm injuries?" For any musculoskeletal issues — use only verbal techniques.
  • Do not use on strangers without context. Hypnotizing a random passerby is a bad idea. Hypnosis is cooperation, not a magic trick. Without context and consent, you risk causing fear, aggression, or trauma.

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 Close Relatives — The Worst Clients for Hypnosis

This is an important and often ignored piece of advice. Jerry warns: attempting to hypnotize close relatives (parents, spouses, children) is almost always doomed to fail.

Why?

  • Their subconscious does not perceive you as an authority in the context of hypnosis — to them you're "son/husband/brother," not "hypnotist."
  • Too much shared context — everyday disputes, relationship history, established roles.
  • Relatives often subconsciously resist — they don't want to "submit" to someone close to them.

Advice: if you want to practice on relatives, do it in a playful manner, without serious therapeutic goals. For real work, find volunteers with whom you have no personal relationship.

🚫 Contraindications

There are conditions in which hypnosis (especially shock and instant techniques) is inadmissible:

  • Epilepsy — shock techniques can trigger a seizure. Even gentle inductions can sometimes provoke epileptiform activity. Always ask about diagnoses before a session.
  • Psychotic disorders — schizophrenia, psychosis, delusional states. Hypnosis can intensify hallucinations or trigger decompensation.
  • Alcohol or drug intoxication — a person in an altered state cannot adequately process suggestions. Additionally, the risk of falls and injuries increases many times over. Absolutely forbidden.
  • Traumatic brain injuries (in medical history) — require caution. Consult a doctor before working.
  • Pregnancy (controversial) — some hypnotists avoid working with pregnant clients due to the risk of premature contractions. If you do work — only gentle, slow techniques.

This list is not exhaustive. If in doubt — don't take risks. Ask the client to consult a doctor before the session.

📝 Practice Assignment

Create a safety checklist:

Create a permanent checklist for yourself that you will use before every session. Here's a basic structure:

✅ Safety Checklist (copy and save)

Before the session:

  • ☐ Informed consent obtained
  • ☐ Session number (1st, 2nd, 3+) — appropriate induction type selected
  • ☐ Asked about injuries (neck, back, arms)
  • ☐ Asked about diagnoses (epilepsy, psychotic disorders)
  • ☐ Alcohol/drug intoxication ruled out
  • ☐ Client is not a close relative (if yes — use playful format)

During the session:

  • ☐ Stance: stable, ready to catch
  • ☐ Balance suggested (when working standing)
  • ☐ Command clear, confident, unambiguous
  • ☐ Immediately after entry — depth check (catalepsy, arm levitation)
  • ☐ Trance deepened before suggestions

After the session:

  • ☐ Full emergence from trance (orientation: name, date, place)
  • ☐ Well-being check (no dizziness, anxiety)
  • ☐ All suggestions removed (especially amnesia, reinduction)
  • ☐ Journal entry: technique, depth, client's reaction

Task: Add your own items to this checklist. Print it out or save it to your notes. Use it before every practice session until it becomes a habit.

4.4 — Post-Hypnotic Suggestions and Reinduction

Module 4 • 15 min

🎬 Video will be inserted after upload to YouTube

🎯 What Is a Post-Hypnotic Suggestion (PHS)

Post-hypnotic suggestion is a command given to a subject in a trance state that is to be carried out after emerging from trance. The subject wakes up, feels normal, but at a specific moment — triggered by a cue — unconsciously executes the embedded instruction.

Real-life example: the hypnotist suggests in trance: "After you open your eyes, when I say the word 'FREEZE,' your body will become motionless, like a statue." The subject comes out of trance, chats, drinks water — then a minute later hears 'FREEZE' and freezes on the spot. At the same time, they are aware of what is happening but cannot resist it.

The key difference between PHS and a regular suggestion in trance: a regular suggestion works while the subject is in trance. PHS carries the effect into the waking state — and triggers automatically, without conscious participation.

⚙️ The Mechanism of PHS

Post-hypnotic suggestion works through a three-part scheme:

  1. The suggestion is given in somnambulism — at a deep level of trance, when the critical factor of the conscious mind is bypassed. The subconscious accepts the command as a directive for action.
  2. Fixation in the subconscious — the suggestion is recorded in the subconscious as a program. It is not consciously perceived, but is stored and awaits activation.
  3. Trigger activation — a stimulus appears in the external environment (word, gesture, sound) that launches the program. The subject performs the action impulsively, often without understanding why they are doing it.

This mechanism is identical to the natural phenomenon that Jerry Kein calls the "law of compounding" — each repetition strengthens the connection between the trigger and the response.

🔄 Post-Hypnotic Reinduction

Post-hypnotic reinduction is a special case of PHS in which the trigger returns the subject back into trance. This is a powerful tool for working with hypnotic phenomena:

Classic reinduction formulation (from the transcript):

"On the count of 3, you will open your eyes, but as soon as I tug my earlobe — you will instantly sink into a trance deeper than you are now. Deeper and deeper each time."

Demonstration from the transcript [264:57–270:17]: The operator suggests a PHS reinduction with a kinesthetic anchor (touching the earlobe). After emerging from trance, the subject feels normal. The operator casually touches their earlobe — and the subject instantly "drops" back into somnambulism, even deeper than the first time.

This shows a remarkable property of reinduction: each subsequent trance induction via the trigger deepens the trance, rather than weakening it.

📋 Requirements for PHS

Post-hypnotic suggestions are not a toy for beginners. For them to work effectively, two conditions are needed:

  • Somnambulism (deep trance): PHS does not work in light or medium trance. The subject must reach at least level 5 on the Davis-Husband scale (ability for spontaneous amnesia). Only in somnambulism does the suggestion become "imprinted" in the subconscious deeply enough to work after awakening.
  • Willingness to participate: PHS will not work if the subject subconsciously resists. If a person does not want to be "programmed" — they will reject the suggestion. Therefore, always obtain conscious consent before working and explain what you are going to do.

How to check for somnambulism: the simplest test is to check for amnesia. If after emerging from trance the subject does not remember what happened (or remembers fragmentarily) — the depth is sufficient. Additionally: check for catalepsy and spontaneous analgesia.

❌ Removing PHS — The Cancel Command

Every post-hypnotic suggestion must have a cancel command. This is a matter of safety and professional ethics.

Why this is important:

  • PHS can keep triggering for years if not specifically removed. Imagine: you suggested that at the word "RELAX" the subject sits on the floor. Six months later, at a meeting, someone says that word — and the person ends up on the floor.
  • Some PHS can be dangerous (e.g., stopping motor activity while driving).
  • The subject has the right to full control over their body and behavior after the session.

How to remove PHS: put the subject back into trance and give a clear cancel suggestion. Example: "The word FREEZE no longer affects you. You are free from this suggestion. If you hear that word, it will have no meaning for you." Or use a universal command: "All suggestions given in this session are now annulled. You are free from all suggestions except those that I specifically name now."

Professional rule: keep a record of all PHS you have given in a session. At the end of the session, go through the list and remove each one if it is not meant to stay (for example, therapeutic suggestions for confidence may remain).

📈 The Law of Compounding with PHS

The law of compounding (compounding — accumulation, reinforcement) is one of the most important principles of hypnosis according to Kein's method. In the context of PHS, it means: each repetition strengthens the suggestion.

  • If you give a PHS once — it may weaken after a few days.
  • If you repeat the suggestion in three different sessions — it becomes fixed for months.
  • If you use reinduction — each subsequent trance via the trigger not only returns the trance but also deepens it, and at the same time reinforces all suggestions given in previous sessions.

Practical application: don't try to pack all necessary suggestions into one session. Break them down over several sessions. Each session reinforces past PHS and adds new ones. After 3–5 sessions, the subject develops a stable program that works without conscious effort.

Caution: the law of compounding also works in reverse. If you accidentally suggest something undesirable — each repetition will reinforce that mistake. Therefore, check each PHS immediately after emerging from trance. If it didn't work, don't leave it as is — redo it.

📝 Practice Assignment

Task 1. PHS for a simple action

Put your partner into deep trance. Give the suggestion: "After you open your eyes and I say the word 'SCRATCH,' you will feel a slight itch in the area of your left palm and you will scratch it." Emerge from trance. After 2–3 minutes, when your partner is distracted, say "SCRATCH." If your partner scratches their palm — PHS worked. Record the result.

Task 2. Reinduction

In deep trance, give the suggestion: "On the count of 3, you will open your eyes, but when I touch your shoulder — your eyes will close by themselves, and you will sink deeper than you are now." Emerge on the count of 3. After a minute, touch their shoulder. If your partner drops into trance — reinduction is working. Note the depth of the trance.

Task 3. Removing PHS

After completing Task 1, put your partner back into trance and remove the PHS: "The word SCRATCH no longer causes any itching. It is an ordinary word; it has no power over your body." Emerge. Check: say "SCRATCH" — there should be no reaction. Note whether the removal went smoothly.

Task 4. Observing the Law of Compounding

Repeat Task 1 a day later with the same partner and the same PHS. Compare the speed and intensity of the reaction. If the reaction strengthened — you are observing the law of compounding. Record in your journal.

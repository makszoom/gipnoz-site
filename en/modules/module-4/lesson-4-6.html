Module 4 • 15 min

🕰️ What Is Regression

Hypnotic regression is a return of the subject to the past, to the origin of a psychotrauma or problematic state. Not to a memory of the past, but to a reliving of the past — with the same emotional intensity, bodily sensations, and even the voice of that age.

From the transcript [301:44–304:16]: The mechanism of regression is explained: "We don't ask 'remember what happened.' We raise a feeling — anxiety, fear, resentment — and allow that feeling to lead us back, to the very first time that feeling arose. This is not a memory — it is a reliving."

The essence of regression is to find the root of the problem not in current behavior, but in past experience. The trauma was fixed in the subconscious a long time ago and continues to affect the person today, often without their understanding why.

⚖️ The Difference Between Direct Suggestion and Regression

These are fundamentally different approaches, and confusing them is one of the most common mistakes among beginner hypnotists.

Direct suggestion (Module 2):

  • Works with the symptom — the current state or behavior
  • Example: "You will feel calm and confident every day."
  • Effect: temporary. If there is no psychotrauma — it can be permanent. If there is a psychotrauma — the suggestion will weaken after a few days or weeks because the root (trauma) has not been addressed.
  • Fast: 1–3 sessions
  • Safe: does not uncover traumatic material

Regression:

  • Works with the cause — past traumatic experience
  • Example: "Allow this feeling of anxiety to lead you back, to the very first time..."
  • Effect: permanent. Once the trauma is found and neutralized, the symptom disappears on its own — without direct suggestions.
  • Slow: requires several sessions, preliminary preparation, and caution
  • Risky: may uncover intense emotions, cause retraumatization

Simple analogy: imagine a person has a splinter in their finger. Direct suggestion is a painkiller: the pain goes away temporarily, but the splinter remains. Regression is removing the splinter: painful at the moment of the procedure, but then the splinter never bothers you again.

🔍 How to Determine if Regression Is Needed

Not every problem requires regression. Here is the criterion used in practice:

Main marker: if in response to your question about the problem, the person starts telling a STORY — "when I was 5 years old...", "it started back in school...", "I remember when my mom told me..." — most likely, there is a psychotrauma, and regression will be more effective.

From the transcript [327:32–330:38]: A demonstration: a client comes in complaining of "fear of public speaking." The operator asks: "When did you first feel this fear?" The client: "I was 7, I was performing at a school event, and everyone laughed when I forgot the words..." — this is a marker. The client is not just afraid of the stage — they have a fixed trauma from the past. Direct suggestion ("you will be calm on stage") will remove the symptom for a couple of weeks but will not eliminate it permanently. Regression to the moment at age 7, neutralization of that trauma — and the fear disappears for good.

When direct suggestion works:

  • The problem is superficial: "can't concentrate," "want more energy"
  • No history — the person simply describes a state without linking it to the past
  • The problem is situational: a reaction to a specific event (e.g., stress after being laid off)
  • The person says: "I don't know where this came from, it's just there" — this is a sign of no obvious trauma

Decision-making algorithm:

  1. Listen to the complaint
  2. Ask: "When did you first feel this?" or "How long ago did this start?"
  3. If the answer is a date/event from the past (especially childhood) → regression is needed
  4. If the answer is "I don't know," "always," "it's just there" → try direct suggestion
  5. If direct suggestion has not produced a lasting effect after 3 sessions → switch to regression

📚 Why Regression Is a Separate Topic (9-Hour Masterclass)

Regression is not just one of the techniques of hypnosis. It is a separate field, to which a 9-hour masterclass is dedicated in the full program. Why so much time?

  1. Safety techniques in regression — the largest block. Regression uncovers suppressed traumas. Improper conduct can retraumatize the client, trigger a psychotic episode, or create false memories. You need to know: how to stabilize emotions, how not to "fall in" yourself, how to work with dissociation, when to stop.
  2. Trauma neutralization — simply finding the trauma is not enough. You need to know how to "rewrite" it, neutralize it, give resources to the past self. There are several protocols: the affective bridge, rewriting the script, inner child, parts integration.
  3. Verification and progression — after neutralization, you need to check if the trauma is gone (return the client to the trigger situation — there should be no reaction). And then — progression: suggesting a desired state in the future (how the client will feel in a month, a year).

This lesson is only an introduction, so you understand when regression is needed and where to go next. Full-fledged practice of regression is beyond the scope of this basic course.

🔗 Connection with Modules 1–3: Why Somnambulism Is Mandatory

For regression, somnambulism is MANDATORY — deep trance of level 5+ on the Davis-Husband scale. This is exactly what you learned in Modules 1–3:

  • Module 1 (rapport and suggestibility): you learned to establish contact, read reactions, calibrate the subject's state. This is the foundation for any hypnotic work.
  • Module 2 (direct suggestion): you learned to formulate and deliver suggestions. This skill will be useful in regression, but by itself, without trance depth, it is ineffective for trauma.
  • Module 3 (somnambulism): you learned to induce deep trance, maintain it, and deepen it. Without somnambulism, regression is impossible. In a superficial trance, a person cannot go into the past deeply enough to relive the trauma. They will simply remember, not regress.

Check yourself: can you consistently elicit catalepsy? Spontaneous amnesia? Post-hypnotic suggestions? If not — go back to lessons 4.1–4.4 and practice these skills. Regression without somnambulism is not regression — it's just conversation under hypnosis.

🚫 You Cannot Do Regression on Yourself

This is a strict rule of hypnotic regression:

  • You cannot simultaneously be the operator and the subject of regression.
  • If you try to conduct regression on yourself, you will either not go deep enough (your conscious mind will control the process), or, if you do go deep — you will lose control of the process and may get "stuck" in a traumatic memory with no way out.
  • Regression requires an external observer who monitors reactions, holds the "safety anchor," and can interrupt the process at any moment.
  • Even experienced hypnotists do not do regression on themselves — they have colleagues for that.

What to do instead of self-regression:

  • Find a partner for mutual practice (you do regression on them, they do it on you)
  • Work with a professional hypnotherapist if you have traumas that require regression
  • For self-development, use direct suggestion, meditation, journaling — but not regression

Your answer: ___________________

Client 3: "I want to quit smoking. I've tried many times, but I relapse. I can't say there's some story from the past connected to it — it's just a habit that I don't like."

Your answer: ___________________

Client 4: "I can't trust men. Every time I start a relationship, I expect betrayal. I remember when I was 16, my first boyfriend betrayed me — he told my secrets to everyone at school. Since then, I can't open up to anyone."

Your answer: ___________________

Client 5: "I have low self-esteem. I can't say there was a specific event — I've just always felt not good enough. It especially shows at work."

Your answer: ___________________

Answer key (check yourself):

Client 1: Direct suggestion. No history, no link to the past. "Don't remember when it started" — marker of no conscious trauma. Try direct suggestion "calmness at heights."

Client 2: Regression. Specific trauma from 3rd grade. The person tells a story. Direct suggestion will remove the fear temporarily, but it will return. Regression to the moment in the classroom is needed.

Client 3: Direct suggestion. No history, no trauma. A habit is something that is well treated with direct suggestion and reinforcement (post-hypnotic suggestions).

Client 4: Regression. Specific trauma at age 16. There is a story, a link to an event. Regression to the moment of betrayal — and neutralization of the trauma.

Client 5: Direct suggestion. "Always felt" — no specific story, no single traumatic event. Direct suggestion for confidence + resource anchors.

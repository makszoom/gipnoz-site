🧩 Four Constructions — The Alphabet of Suggestion

Once you know what to suggest (the answers to the three questions from lesson 5.1), you need to know how to suggest it. Four universal linguistic constructions are identified that embed into direct suggestions. They work at different subconscious levels and can be combined with each other.

Here are the four constructions:

  1. Presupposition of awareness — "you will notice, see, realize"
  2. Presupposition of degree — "how much more confident, how much easier"
  3. The "the more → the more" link — "the deeper you go, the better you feel"
  4. "Since / because" and "as if / as though" — reason and softening

Let's break each one down in detail with examples.

👁️ Construction 1 — Presupposition of Awareness

Formula: "You will notice / see / realize / feel that..."

This construction presupposes that the change is already happening — the client just hasn't noticed it yet. You don't say "you will become confident" — you say "you will notice how confidence appears in your voice." The difference is that the first is a demand (causes resistance), while the second is a statement of the inevitable.

Examples:

  • "And you notice how your breathing becomes more and more even and calm..."
  • "You realize that your body chooses relaxation on its own, without effort..."
  • "And you will see how familiar fears lose their power, becoming just thoughts that come and go..."
  • "You will feel how that very calmness we talked about appears inside..."

Why it works: the subconscious perceives this as a description of an already existing process. The critical factor lets it through — after all, you're not asserting, you're simply suggesting noticing.

📊 Construction 2 — Presupposition of Degree

Formula: "How much more confident / calm / easy / simple..."

Here the presupposition is built into the word "how much" or its equivalents. You're not asking "will it be better?" — you're asking "how much better?" The subconscious accepts the fact of improvement and starts looking for an answer to the question "how much."

Examples:

  • "And you can already feel how much calmer your breathing has become..."
  • "Notice how much easier your body feels..."
  • "You'll be surprised how much simpler it is now to make decisions..."
  • "Notice how much more confident your inner voice sounds..."

Variants: "more and more," "deeper and deeper," "calmer and calmer." Double comparison amplifies the effect.

Example: "With each breath you feel more and more confident, calmer and calmer inside..."

Why it works: the words "how much" and "more and more" create a frame within which the change is already happening. The conscious mind doesn't dispute the frame — it fills it with content.

🔄 Construction 3 — The "the more → the more" Link

Formula: "The more [action/state], the more [result]"

This is a cause-and-effect link that creates an automatic dependency: one thing naturally reinforces the other. The subconscious accepts this link as a given.

Examples:

  • "The deeper you go into this state, the better you feel..."
  • "The more you relax, the clearer your mind becomes..."
  • "The calmer your breathing, the more confident you feel..."
  • "The more often you notice this confidence, the stronger it becomes..."

Advanced variant: link the hypnotic state with the desired result.

"The deeper you are in trance right now, the easier it is for your subconscious to accept the new state of confidence and grounding. And the stronger this state enters you, the deeper and more comfortable the trance becomes..."

Why it works: creates a cyclic reinforcement. Trance deepens the suggestion, suggestion deepens the trance — a positive feedback loop.

🧠 Construction 4 — "Since / because" and "as if / as though"

Formula 1 — reason: "[Suggestion], since / because [reason]"

Adding a reason makes the suggestion more logical and easier to accept. Even if the reason isn't logical — the "because" format itself eases passage through the critical factor.

Examples:

  • "Your body relaxes because it knows that now is the time for rest and recovery..."
  • "You feel confidence because this state is natural for you — it was simply hidden and is now coming to light..."
  • "You allow yourself to let go of control, since your subconscious knows what to do..."

Formula 2 — as if: "As if / as though [the desired state has already arrived]"

This construction softens the suggestion, removes direct pressure and analytical resistance. You don't say "you are confident now" (which may cause inner protest: "no, I'm not!"). You say "as if you already feel this confidence..." — the subconscious accepts the play.

Examples:

  • "And you can imagine, as if you're already standing before that audience and feeling complete calm..."
  • "As though the entire burden you've been carrying has simply dissolved..."
  • "As if that very grounding we talked about has lit up inside you..."
  • "And you notice how everything falls into place by itself, as if..."

Why it works: "as if" bypasses the critical factor through metaphor and play. The subconscious starts looking for the "as if" feeling and discovers that it's already there.

🔧 Building a Suggestion: Example for "Confidence"

Now let's assemble all four constructions into one coherent suggestion. Client's request: "I want confidence and grounding so I can calmly say 'no' without feeling guilty."

📜 Example of a finished suggestion (2–3 minute fragment in trance)

"And now, as you are in this deep, calm state... you notice (presupposition of awareness) how your breathing becomes more and more even... how your shoulders drop and relax on their own...

And you can pay attention to how much calmer (presupposition of degree) it feels inside... how much easier it is to breathe...

The deeper you go into this calm, the more clearly (the more→the more) you feel that very confidence and grounding inside yourself... it was always there, just now it's waking up...

And you allow yourself to feel this confidence, because (since) it's your natural state... the state of a person who knows what matters to them...

And you can now imagine, as if (as though) you are already saying 'no' calmly and without guilt... notice how easily it comes... how steady your voice sounds... how calm your body is...

And with each breath this confidence and grounding enter you deeper... and with each exhale, everything unnecessary leaves... and you notice how this new state becomes yours... natural... familiar..."

Notice: all four constructions are embedded into a smooth flow of speech. They don't look like "techniques" — they sound like a simple description of what is happening.

📋 How to Combine Constructions

In a single suggestion, you can use all four constructions sequentially or mix them. Recommended structure:

  1. Start with a presupposition of awareness ("you notice...")
  2. Strengthen with a presupposition of degree ("how much easier...")
  3. Link with trance through "the more→the more"
  4. Justify through "since/because" and soften through "as if"

Important: don't try to cram all four constructions into every sentence. Build the suggestion so it sounds natural. Constructions are a tool, not a template.

✍️ Practice: Create a Suggestion Using All 4 Constructions

Exercise 1. Take this request: "I want to feel calm and confident when talking to my boss. I'll know it's working because my voice won't tremble, and I'll be able to express my thoughts clearly. Why? So that I'm respected and heard."

Create a suggestion using all four constructions. Write it as a 2–3 minute script.

Exercise 2 — pair work. One is the client (names any topic for suggestion). The second is the therapist — in 5 minutes, composes and delivers a suggestion using at least 3 of the 4 constructions.

Exercise 3 — analysis. Read your script and mark each construction with a color. Did you find room for all four? Which construction came most easily? Which was the hardest?

Success criterion: The suggestion sounds natural and smooth. If you or your partner notice "artificiality" or "template-ness" — rewrite it, rearranging the order of constructions and using different wording.

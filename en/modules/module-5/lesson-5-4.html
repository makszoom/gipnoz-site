5.4 — Direct Suggestion vs Regression

Module 5 • 15 min

🎬 Video will be inserted after uploading to YouTube

🔍 The Main Question: Direct Suggestion or Regression?

Not every request can be solved with direct suggestion. It is emphasized: there are two fundamentally different types of problems, and each has its own tool. Choosing the wrong tool is the most costly mistake a hypnotherapist can make.

The key criterion is the presence of psychotrauma. If the client has deep trauma (especially from childhood), direct suggestion will only give a temporary effect. If there's no trauma — direct suggestion can solve the problem permanently.

How to tell the difference? A simple and precise history test is given.

🧪 The History Test

Ask the client: "In your opinion, what's the problem?"

And listen to how they answer.

  • If the person tells a story: "When I was 5 years old...", "My father left when I was little...", "I was constantly teased at school..." — there is psychotrauma. Regression is needed.
  • If the person describes their current state or behavior: "I can't say no to people," "I get nervous before presentations," "I want to manage my time better" — surface problem. Direct suggestion works.

Psychotrauma isn't necessarily something catastrophic (violence, loss of parents). It can be "just" constant devaluation in childhood, parents' divorce, bullying. If the client has an emotional charge on a past event — it's trauma. If not — it's a pattern, habit, or belief.

Important: don't conduct regression if you're not trained in it (that's a separate 9-hour masterclass). Your job as a direct suggestion specialist is to diagnose correctly and give the client an appropriate response: "You need something else — and I can help you prepare for it."

⏳ Direct Suggestion — Temporary Effect with Trauma

A vivid example from practice. A client came in who 7 years earlier had a direct suggestion session with another hypnotist. For 7 years (!) everything worked perfectly. Then it came back. She came with the same problem.

Why did it come back? Because she had psychotrauma that the direct suggestion hadn't touched. It put a "patch" on the symptom, but the root remained. The patch held for 7 years — which speaks to the power of suggestion. But the root wasn't processed, and over time the symptom grew back.

It took 5 regression sessions to solve the problem permanently.

Conclusion: direct suggestion for psychotrauma is a crutch. Effective, sometimes very long-lasting, but not a cure.

✅ When Direct Suggestion Works

Direct suggestion is the ideal tool for surface problems without deep roots. What are these problems?

  • Habits: smoking, overeating, nail-biting (if there's no traumatic background)
  • Confidence in specific situations: presentations, interviews, first dates
  • Sports performance: improving concentration, removing pre-race jitters
  • Sleep and relaxation: improving sleep quality, relieving chronic tension
  • "Stuck" states: procrastination, laziness, lack of motivation
  • Communication: ability to say "no," public speaking, ease in conversation

Key indicator: the client doesn't tell a story. They simply want to change current behavior. It's like reinstalling a program — without formatting the hard drive.

⚠️ Distinction: Surface Problem vs Trauma

Summary table of differences:

  • Surface problem: describes current state → "I'm shy talking to tall people" → no emotional history → direct suggestion resolves in 1–3 sessions
  • Psychotrauma: tells a story → "I was teased at school because of my height" → there's an emotional charge → direct suggestion gives temporary effect, regression needed

Nuance: there are mixed cases. A person comes with a surface request, but during diagnosis trauma "surfaces." In such cases:

  1. Do direct suggestion for the current request — this will take the edge off and give the client experience of trance.
  2. In the second session, discuss that full resolution requires regression.
  3. Refer the client to a regressionist or, if you're trained, conduct regression yourself.

🎯 Why Do Direct Suggestion if There's Trauma?

It might seem: if the client has trauma and direct suggestion won't permanently solve the problem, why do it at all? Answer: so the client believes hypnosis works.

There are three reasons to conduct a direct suggestion session even when you see trauma:

  1. Hope. A client who has lived with a problem for years is often skeptical. If they experience even temporary relief, they'll believe in the tool.
  2. Demonstration. One successful direct suggestion session shows the client that their subconscious can change. This breaks the "I'm hopeless" frame.
  3. Preparation for regression. A client who has already been in trance and knows how it works will enter a regressive state much deeper and faster. You shorten future session time.

Moral principle: always tell the client the truth. Don't promise "forever" if you see trauma. Say: "We have two paths. The first is direct suggestion — it will give a quick result but may be temporary. The second is regression — deeper work that will take more sessions but will resolve the root. We can start with the first and see how it goes." This is honest and professional.

✍️ Practice: Analyze 3 Cases

Instructions: For each case, determine whether direct suggestion is sufficient or regression is needed. Justify your answer.

Case 1. Elena, 34. Came with the request "I want to stop snapping at my husband." When asked "what's the problem?" she answers: "I'm just very tired from work, I have no energy, and I snap at loved ones. I want to learn to react calmly." She doesn't tell stories from childhood.

Your diagnosis: ______________

Case 2. Mikhail, 28. "I'm afraid to approach girls." When asked about the problem, he says: "I was teased at school about being overweight, and I still feel insecure. When I try to approach someone, I hear that same laughter inside." He speaks about this with visible tension.

Your diagnosis: ______________

Case 3. Anna, 41. "I want to quit smoking." Has been smoking for 15 years. When asked "why do you smoke?" she answers: "Habit. Relieves stress. I know it's bad, but I can't stop." No memories associated with smoking. Describes her childhood as ordinary.

Your diagnosis: ______________

Debrief after practice:

  • Case 1 (Elena): Direct suggestion. No story, no trauma. Work with the state of calm and reactions.
  • Case 2 (Mikhail): Regression. There's a story (school bullying), emotional charge. Direct suggestion may give temporary relief, but the root remains.
  • Case 3 (Anna): Direct suggestion. No traumatic background. Work with habit and substitution.

Success criterion: You can determine within 2 minutes of conversation whether the client has psychotrauma or not, and choose the tool accordingly. If after 5 minutes of conversation you still don't know — return to the history test.

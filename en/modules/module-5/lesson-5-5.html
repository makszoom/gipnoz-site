🏗️ Complete Structure of the First Session

The first session is the most important. It's the moment when the client either becomes convinced hypnosis works, or gets disappointed and leaves. A clear, proven structure is offered that minimizes risks and maximizes results.

Here are all the stages of the first session in order:

  1. Diagnosis — information gathering, three questions (5.1)
  2. Pre-talk — removing fears, the model of consciousness
  3. Permissions — using first name, touch, readiness
  4. Full Elman induction — from Module 1
  5. Eyelid catalepsy — demonstrating trance depth to the client
  6. Direct suggestion — 10–15 minutes minimum (from 5.2, 5.3)
  7. Emergence — count 1–3
  8. Post-hypnotic window — 30–90 seconds of silence

Let's break each stage down in detail.

1️⃣ Diagnosis

The first thing you do when meeting a client is not trance induction, but conversation. Diagnosis is the stage where you gather information, formulate the task, and determine whether regression is needed.

Minimum diagnostic protocol:

  • Three questions from lesson 5.1 (WHAT, HOW, WHY)
  • History test from lesson 5.4
  • 0–10 scale on current state
  • Recording the client's key words (for future suggestion)

Time: 10–15 minutes. Don't drag out the diagnosis — the client came for a solution, not to tell their life story. Answers to the three questions are enough to start.

2️⃣ Pre-talk

Pre-talk is a short conversation before induction that removes fears and aligns expectations. Many clients are afraid of hypnosis: afraid of losing control, not waking up, revealing secrets, "becoming a puppet."

What to include in pre-talk:

  • Model of consciousness: "Hypnosis is not sleep or unconsciousness. It's a state of deep relaxation and focus. You will hear everything I say. If something goes wrong, you can open your eyes at any moment."
  • Control: "You are completely in control of the process. Your subconscious only accepts what is beneficial for you. If a suggestion doesn't suit you, it will simply pass by."
  • Normalization: "Everything you feel is normal. Lightness, heaviness, warmth, tingling, drowsiness — these are simply signs of relaxation."

Time: 3–5 minutes. Brief, clear, without unnecessary explanations.

3️⃣ Permissions

Before induction, get explicit permissions from the client:

  • First name basis: "Do you mind if I use your first name? In hypnosis it helps to relax and reduces the distance." (Some clients prefer a formal address — respect their choice.)
  • Touch: "Sometimes I'll touch your shoulder or wrist to deepen relaxation. Is that okay?"
  • Readiness: "Are you ready to begin? You don't have any urgent business for the next hour? Phone off?"

Permissions are not a formality. They create a safe space in which the client fully relaxes. Without permission, any touch could be perceived as a boundary violation.

4️⃣ Full Elman Induction

After diagnosis, pre-talk, and permissions — directly to induction. The full Elman induction (from Module 1) is recommended as the baseline. Why Elman specifically?

  • It's proven by decades of practice
  • Gives reliable trance depth
  • Includes eyelid catalepsy as a built-in check
  • Works with almost all clients

The induction takes 5–10 minutes. Don't rush, take pauses, watch the client's reactions.

5️⃣ Eyelid Catalepsy — Demonstration for the Client

After induction, before direct suggestion, it's recommended to show the client eyelid catalepsy. Why? So the client sees and feels that they are in hypnosis.

Many clients in trance doubt: "Is this really hypnosis? Maybe I just relaxed?" Eyelid catalepsy is visual proof. When the client tries to open their eyes and can't, they receive a sensory experience of trance, not just a suggestion.

Technique: "Now I'm going to ask you to try to open your eyes. Don't strain — just try. You'll find that your eyelids don't obey — they're stuck, heavy, relaxed so deeply they don't want to open. Try... and let go. That's it. Now you know you're in hypnosis."

6️⃣ Direct Suggestion — 10–15 Minutes Minimum

Minimum time for direct suggestion in the first session — 10–15 minutes. Ideally — 30–60 minutes (see compounding, lesson 5.3).

What to suggest? What you found out during diagnosis: using the client's words, the four constructions (5.2), and the principle of compounding (5.3).

In the first session, don't experiment. Stick to one theme — the one you agreed on with the client during diagnosis. Don't jump to related topics. One session — one task.

7️⃣ Emergence (Count 1–3)

Ending trance — a gentle and gradual emergence. A simple count is used:

"I'm going to count from one to three now. On three, you'll open your eyes, feeling rested and refreshed. One — your breath deepens, the sensation of your body returns. Two — you hear sounds around you, feel the surface of the chair. Three — take a deep breath, open your eyes, stretch."

If the client doesn't open their eyes on "three" — pause 5–10 seconds, then repeat the count more slowly.

8️⃣ Post-hypnotic Window — 30–90 Seconds of Silence

This stage is often skipped by beginners — and it's a mistake. After emerging from trance, the client is in a post-hypnotic window: a state of heightened suggestibility that lasts 30 to 90 seconds.

Don't say anything during this time! Don't ask "how are you?", don't comment. Just be silent. Let the suggestion "settle."

If you start talking immediately after emergence, you'll "overwrite" the post-hypnotic effect. Your silence is the completion of the suggestion. The client integrates the changes themselves during these seconds.

After 30–90 seconds (gauge by the client — when they start to move, take a deep breath, look around), you can begin talking.

💰 Work Frame and Client Responsibility

It is emphasized: a professional hypnotherapist is not just "a person who gives suggestions." They are a specialist with clear boundaries and structure.

Paid diagnosis filters out the unprepared. The first consultation (diagnosis) should be paid. This is a filter: if a person isn't willing to pay for diagnosis, they aren't ready to work on themselves. Free diagnosis attracts "tourists" who just want to watch but don't intend to change.

Client responsibility: It's important to establish from the very beginning: "I can help you if you follow my instructions." This isn't about being harsh — it's about a contract. The client must understand that 50% of success depends on them (readiness, trust, following post-session recommendations).

Rule "professionalism = speed": Don't drag out the session. Work quickly, clearly, without unnecessary words. If diagnosis took 15 minutes — good. If pre-talk took 3 minutes — excellent. The client values their time, not chatter.

🔄 Transition to Regression

If after the first session you see the problem is deeper and requires regression, the structure changes:

  • First session: direct suggestion (as described above). The client gets experience of hypnosis and temporary relief.
  • Second session and beyond: regression. Finding the root cause, re-experiencing, integration.

Important: don't try regression in the first session. The client doesn't have trust or experience yet. The first session is always demonstration and preparation.

A client who needs regression can either be referred to a specialist (if you're not trained) or you can conduct it yourself (if you've been trained in regressive hypnosis). Don't take on regression without preparation — it's harder than direct suggestion and requires separate skills.

✍️ Practice: Create a First Session Plan

Assignment. Create a detailed plan for a first session with a fictional client using the following scenario:

Client: Ivan, 35. Request: "I want to feel confident when talking to management. Right now I tense up, my voice trembles, I'm afraid of saying something stupid. I'll know the problem is solved when I can calmly express my opinion at meetings. Why — so I'm taken seriously and can grow in my career." On the history test: "No, nothing like that in childhood. I'm just an introvert."

Write it out by stages:

  1. Diagnosis: What questions will you ask? What client words will you write in your notebook?
  2. Pre-talk: What specifically will you say? What fears will you address?
  3. Permissions: What specific permissions will you ask for?
  4. Type of induction: Which induction will you choose? (You can reference Module 1)
  5. Key phrase for suggestion: What client words will be the foundation?
  6. Planned suggestion time: How many minutes?
  7. Emergence and post-hypnotic window: How many seconds of silence after emergence?

Success criterion: Your plan is a ready-made scenario you can use to conduct a full first session without improvising or skipping stages. If the plan is too abstract ("talk to the client," "do suggestion") — add specifics.

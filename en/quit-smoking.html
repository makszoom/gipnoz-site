What Is Quit Now?

Quit Now is a Chrome extension that helps you stop smoking by giving you instant relief from nicotine cravings — right from your browser toolbar. It combines a 60-second breathing and anchoring technique with a full 10-minute hypnosis session based on Gerald Kein's clinical smoking cessation protocol.

The extension also tracks your progress: days smoke-free, money saved, cigarettes not smoked, and life regained. Every craving you beat is logged. Milestones from 1 day to 1 year keep you motivated.

How It Works

The technique is based on two principles from clinical hypnosis: breathing regulation and Pavlovian anchoring. When a craving hits, you open the extension and follow a 60-second exercise that activates your parasympathetic nervous system while installing a physical trigger (pressing thumb and forefinger together) associated with calm determination.

Step 1 — Breathe (12 seconds)

The craving is a wave. It rises, peaks, and falls. You don't have to act on it. Follow the breathing guide to activate your vagus nerve.

Step 2 — Anchor (12 seconds)

Press your thumb and forefinger together. This is your freedom anchor. From now on, this gesture triggers calm determination.

Step 3 — Reframe (24 seconds)

You are not giving up anything. You are gaining freedom, health, and control. The cigarette was never your friend — it was a transaction.

Step 4 — Future-Pace (12 seconds)

See yourself as a non-smoker. The wave has passed. You rode it. You are getting stronger.

Instant Craving Relief (60 seconds)

Why It Works

Hypnosis works for smoking cessation by addressing the subconscious patterns that keep you smoking — the triggers, the rituals, the "just one more" voice. Conscious willpower fights these patterns; hypnosis rewrites them.

A 2007 meta-analysis in the International Journal of Clinical and Experimental Hypnosis found hypnosis more effective than nicotine replacement therapy for smoking cessation. The technique works because smoking is primarily a subconscious behavior — you don't consciously choose to crave a cigarette.

The freedom anchor creates a conditioned response: pressing your fingers together becomes associated with calm determination. The more you use it, the stronger it gets. Each craving you ride out weakens the neural pathway. Each one you give in to strengthens it.

What is the freedom anchor?

The freedom anchor is a conditioned response technique from clinical hypnosis. By pressing your thumb and forefinger together while in a state of calm determination, you create an association. After repetition, the gesture alone triggers the state — useful when a craving hits and you need immediate relief.

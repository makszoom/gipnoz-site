Positive Hallucination

🔬 Hypnosis phenomena

📋 When to Use

Positive hallucination — suggesting an image to the subject that objectively does not exist — is the highest level of hypnotic artistry. It is used to demonstrate the deep stage of trance (somnambulism), when the subconscious is so powerful that it can "paint" reality for the conscious mind. On stage, this creates a sensation: the person "sees" a non-existent object, picks it up, describes it. In therapy, positive hallucination is used for resource states — the subject can "see" their inner mentor, protector, or ideal place of power. The technique requires prior achievement of deep trance and good rapport.

📝 Script

Preparation and Entry into Deep Trance

Positive hallucination requires a somnambulistic depth of trance. Use any induction that produces a deep state — shock, progressive, confusion. Make sure the subject has passed the eyelid and hand catalepsy test. Depth check: "Now I will tell you — and you won't be able to say your name. Try... see? Your subconscious is blocking the answer. You are deep inside." If the subject cannot remember or say their name — the depth is sufficient for hallucination.

Step 1. Suggestive Preparation for Hallucination

"Now you will open your eyes, but remain in a deep trance. Your subconscious will take control of your vision. You will see not what is actually there, but what I tell you to see. And what you see will be absolutely real to you. You will be able to touch it, describe it, and even smell or taste it. Your subconscious will create a complete reality for you... Ready? Open your eyes and look straight ahead."

Step 2. Creating the Hallucination — Apple

The subject sits with open eyes, gaze slightly unfocused. You extend your hand (without holding anything) and act as if you're holding something on your palm. "Look at my palm. You see an apple. Gradually it appears... first the outline... a soft, green apple... do you see it? It's appearing right now... The skin is smooth, with small stripes. There's a little leaf on the stem. Look closely — do you see the leaf? The apple is becoming clearer, more real. It's here. You can take it in your hands. Reach out and take it."

Step 3. Interactive Check — Manipulating the Object

Observe the subject. If they reach for your imaginary apple — the hallucination is working. Continue: "Take it in your palm. Feel its weight — it's firm, but not too heavy. The skin is smooth and cool to the touch. Run your fingers over the surface... see how it shines? Bring it to your nose and smell it — feel the fresh, fruity aroma... Now take a bite... Hear the crunch... Feel the juice filling your mouth, the sweet-sour taste on your tongue... Swallow. The taste lingers in your mouth, refreshing, natural. You truly feel this — because for your subconscious, this is reality."

Step 4. Removing the Hallucination

"Now finish the apple if you like, or simply throw it away. I will count to three — and the apple will disappear, as if it was never there. One — it begins to dissolve in the air... Two — only a faint memory remains... Three — the apple has completely disappeared. In front of you is an empty hand again. Close your eyes and return to deep trance."

Step 5. More Complex Hallucination — A Person in the Room

If the subject successfully passed the apple test, move to a more complex task. "Now open your eyes and look at the corner of the room (point in a direction). There is a person standing there. Do you see them? They are dressed in a light suit. They are smiling at you. They are a friend — they are here to support you. Look closely at their face — do you recognize them? Can you describe what they look like? What color is their hair? How old are they? They want to tell you something — listen... They are saying to you... (pause — give the subject time to hear). Good. Now they wave at you and slowly disappear. They are leaving, but their support stays with you. One more breath — and they are gone. Close your eyes. Deep breath in and out. You are back in the silence of trance."

Step 6. Positive Hallucination for Therapy

Turn the hallucination into a therapeutic tool: "Now you can create a place of power in your imagination. Imagine you are in a place of absolute peace and safety. Look around — what do you see? Maybe it's an ocean shore? Or a forest clearing? Or a room filled with sunlight? (Give 10–15 seconds). Remember all the details of this place. Colors, sounds, smells. Now create a power object in this place — it could be a glowing sphere, a crystal, or any shape you like. Take it in your hands. Feel its energy. This energy is your own inner strength, knowledge, and wisdom. Do you feel the warmth emanating from it? It fills every cell of your body. Now you can always return to this place whenever you want to feel peace and strength. Close your eyes, keeping this feeling inside. Deep breath in... out..."

Advanced Work: Tactile and Auditory Hallucination

Positive hallucinations are not limited to vision. You can evoke tactile sensations: "I place a hot coin on your palm. Do you feel the heat?" — and the subject will actually feel heat in their empty hand. Or auditory: "Now you will hear the sound of a bell — it will ring until I say 'stop'." Important: progress from simple to complex. First, a visual hallucination of a simple object (apple, ball), then a complex one (person), then multimodal (see+hear+touch). Not all subjects are capable of multimodal hallucinations on the first try — that's normal.

Diagnostic Value

Positive hallucination is a reliable indicator of the somnambulistic stage of hypnosis. If the subject "sees" an apple, tries to take it, describes it, smells it, and tastes it — you are dealing with a deep stage of trance. This is a marker that the subconscious dominates the conscious, and therapeutic suggestions will be maximally effective. Record which modalities work for each subject — visual, auditory, or kinesthetic. This information will help you select individual suggestions for future sessions.

Exiting Trance After Hallucination

"Now I will begin the reverse count. All hallucinatory images will completely disappear. You will return to your normal state clear, rested, full of energy. On the count of five — full awakening. One — the deep state begins to dissolve, like morning fog... Two — you feel your body, your breathing... Three — energy fills you, you feel lightness and clarity... Four — wiggle your fingers, make a gentle movement with your shoulders, open your eyes... Five — full awakening! You are here. Now. Fresh and rested. How do you feel? What stood out the most?"

Post-Trance Discussion

Carefully discuss the experience. Don't say "you saw a hallucination" — this may cause resistance or fear. Say: "Your imagination was working very vividly, that's a sign of deep subconscious work. Some people get so immersed that they actually see images — this is normal and even beneficial. It means your brain knows how to create internal realities, which opens up enormous possibilities for self-development and healing." If the subject says they didn't see the apple, only "imagined" it — that's also a success. Don't insist. Say: "Great, your visual channel is beginning to open. With practice, the images will become brighter."

Precautions

Positive hallucination is a powerful tool. Do not evoke images that could frighten the subject (scary faces, monsters, dangerous situations). Do not suggest hallucinations of people the subject knows — this can create psychological confusion. After removing the hallucination, always give a clear suggestion that all images have completely disappeared and the subject sees the real room. Make sure the subject does not retain a post-hypnotic hallucination after emerging from trance. If after awakening the subject still claims to see the imaginary object — return them to trance and give a clear command: "The image completely disappears. You see the ordinary room again, like everyone else." Only then bring them out definitively.

💡 Tip

Start with an apple — it's the simplest and most intuitive object. Why an apple? Because every person has kinesthetic memory of an apple: we know what it looks like, smells like, feels like, tastes like. It's a multimodal image that is easy to activate. Never start with abstract or frightening objects. Use your own hand to show "where" the object is — your gesture combined with the absence of a real object creates cognitive dissonance that helps the subject's subconscious fill the void with an image. And remember: if the subject doesn't succeed on the first try — don't be disappointed. Say: "Great attempt! Your conscious mind is still filtering images. Let's try a little differently..." and repeat, adding more sensory details. Persistence and patience are the key.

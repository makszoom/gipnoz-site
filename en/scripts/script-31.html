Amnesia Numbers Phenomenon

31. Amnesia of Numbers, Name, and Event

🌀 Module 4 — Demonstrating Phenomena (lesson 4.3)

When to Use

Hypnotic amnesia is the temporary blocking of access to specific information. It is not memory loss — it is a temporary block that is removed on command. Key fact: amnesia is possible only in somnambulism.

  • Hidden amnesia (numbers) — built into the Elman induction as a test for somnambulism.
  • Open amnesia (name) — demonstration after confirming somnambulism.
  • Event amnesia — for post-hypnotic work and demonstrations.

⏱ Duration: 3–5 minutes per type.

📜 Hidden Number Amnesia (from Elman Induction)

Principle

The person counts backward from 100, adding "more relaxation." After several numbers they "disappear" — the person stops saying them because there's nothing to say. The person doesn't know they're being tested — this makes the test hidden.

Hidden test — the person cannot "play along" or "fear failure."

Script (part of classic Elman)

"Now you're going to count backward from 100… 99… 98… 97… 96…"

"With each number you relax deeper… 95… 94… 93… deeper… more relaxation… 92… 91… 90…"

[The person continues counting. At some point numbers "disappear" — they fall silent.]

"Keep going… count further… even deeper…" [If silent — amnesia worked.]

⚠️ Critical: Never say "all numbers have disappeared." Say: "Everything has disappeared. Gone. Dissolved. Nothing is left." The subconscious takes words literally — the word "numbers" would bring them back.

📜 Open Number Amnesia (Controlled Demonstration)

In deep trance — setting the suggestion

"In a few seconds I will wake you. But first your subconscious will do one thing…"

"When I ask you to count from 1 to 10 — you will count… but one number won't be there. It will vanish. Dissolve. It doesn't exist."

"You will try to remember it — and you won't be able to. The harder you try, the further it slips away."

"One… two… three — eyes open. You're fresh and alert."

Testing

"Count from 1 to 10."

[The person skips a number (usually 6 or 7).]

Removal

"Now I'll snap my fingers — and all numbers will return to their places. Snap! There, all numbers are back. Count again."

📜 Name Amnesia

In deep trance

"In a few seconds I'll count to three. On the count of three you will open your eyes. You won't remember your name."

"The harder you try to remember — the more your name slips away. It's funny — you know you have a name, but you can't remember it. Just for a little while. Want it to happen."

"One, two, three. Eyes open." [Pause] "What's your name?"

[The person freezes. Tries to remember. Cannot.]

"Can't remember? That's normal. Completely normal."

Removal

"Now I'll snap my fingers — and your name will come back. Snap! What's your name? [Person says it] Great. It's back."

⚠️ Important: Name amnesia is a powerful phenomenon. Do not prolong it beyond 10–15 seconds. Can cause anxiety. Always have a return command ready.

📜 Event Amnesia

In deep trance

"What happened in the last [5 minutes / this session] — will vanish from your memory. When you open your eyes, you won't remember anything we did."

"You will remember coming here… you will feel good… but what exactly we did — will disappear. Dissolve."

"Your subconscious will keep all the benefit from our work. But consciously you won't remember what happened."

"One… two… three — eyes open. How do you feel?"

Removal

"Now I'll snap my fingers — and all memories will return. Snap! Now you remember everything we did. Everything is in place."

Check: "What did we do in the last 5 minutes?" If they remember — amnesia is removed.

📜 Aphasia vs Amnesia — How to Tell the Difference

Aphasia: the person cannot speak due to deep relaxation of the jaw. Wants to answer but physically cannot.

Amnesia: the person can speak, but has nothing to say — the information has vanished from consciousness.

How to tell: ask "Is everything gone?"

  • If the person nods — amnesia.
  • If silent — possibly aphasia.

Get verbal or non-verbal confirmation.

💡 Important

  • Amnesia is a marker of somnambulism. If the person cannot forget on your suggestion — they are not in somnambulism. Don't try to force it.
  • Never say "the numbers have disappeared." The word "numbers" brings them back. Say: "everything has disappeared," "gone," "dissolved."
  • Always remove the suggestion. Amnesia must have a return trigger (snap, word, touch). Without removal, the block may persist.
  • Name amnesia — no longer than 10–15 seconds. This can be distressing for the subject.
  • Event amnesia — for advanced practitioners only. Use only if you are confident in trance depth and subject trust.

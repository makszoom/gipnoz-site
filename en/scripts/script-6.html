"Breathe and Sleep"

⚡ Instant inductions

📋 When to Use

When you need a fast trance without lengthy preparation. Ideal for demonstrating the power of hypnosis to a beginner, for the stage, and as a first step before deeper techniques. Works with virtually any subject, including those who "doubt they can be hypnotized." Duration — 10–15 seconds. The technique is taken directly from Jerry Kane's arsenal and the stage hypnotists of the classic hypnosis school.

📝 Script

Preparation

The subject stands or sits in front of you. Establish eye contact, smile. Make sure they are ready to follow your instructions. Say: "I'm going to show you how fast your subconscious works. Just do what I say — and it will work."

Step 1. Command to Inhale

Bring your palm to the subject's face at a distance of 15–20 centimeters — so they see it with their peripheral vision. Say in a firm, confident voice: "Take a deep breath in..." — and at the same time, inhale yourself, leading by example. Draw out the word "deep" so it lasts about two seconds. Your voice should sound like a command, but without aggression — calm and authoritative.

Step 2. Pause on Breath Hold

As soon as the subject begins to inhale, immediately say: "...and hold your breath." Say these words quickly, almost run together. The subject holds the air in their lungs. At this moment, their attention is maximally focused on you — they are waiting for what comes next. Your task is not to let them slip into anticipation, but to use this point of focus. Hold their gaze: look directly into their eyes without breaking away. Pause for 2–3 seconds. During these seconds, the subject's consciousness "freezes" — this is the ideal moment for suggestion.

Step 3. Suggestion on the Exhale

Sharply, but not shouting, in a commanding tone say: "Now exhale — and sleep!" Say the word "exhale" as a command, and the word "sleep" as the final point, with descending intonation. At the moment the subject begins to exhale, you can gently press on their shoulder or touch their forehead — this strengthens the suggestion. Important: don't wait for them to exhale completely. The command "sleep" should sound at the very beginning of the exhale.

Step 4. Accepting the Trance

After the "sleep" command, most subjects' eyes close, their breathing becomes steady, and their body relaxes. If the subject didn't close their eyes — don't panic. Calmly say: "Close your eyes. Deep inside, you are already in trance. Just allow it to happen." Then add: "With every exhale, you sink deeper and deeper... lower and lower... into a state of complete peace."

Step 5. Deepening

As soon as the eyes are closed, begin deepening. Use the rhythm of the subject's breathing: "With every inhale — calmness... with every exhale — deeper... Imagine you're standing on an escalator that's gently going down... ten steps down... nine... eight... with every step you sink deeper into yourself... seven... six... five... halfway there... four... three... deep, peaceful trance... two... one... complete peace. You are at the very bottom. It's quiet here. It's safe here. You hear only my voice."

Step 6. Anchoring the State

Now that the subject is in trance, anchor the state: "Remember this feeling. Inner stillness. Lightness in your body. Your mind is clear and calm. The next time I say 'sleep' — you will fall into this state even faster and deeper. This is an anchor. The word 'sleep' is your entry into trance. Agree with this in your subconscious... good."

Working in Trance

At this stage, you can deliver the necessary suggestions — for confidence, relaxation, habit work, or simply let the subject enjoy deep rest. Duration of work — from 3 to 15 minutes depending on goals. Use direct suggestions, metaphors, or visualizations — whatever is appropriate in your context.

Exiting the Trance

"Now I will begin the countdown. On the count of five, you will open your eyes and return here fully awake, rested, and full of energy. One — a deep wave of relaxation recedes, feel the lightness returning to your body... Two — energy begins to fill every cell... Three — wiggle your fingers and toes, take a gentle breath... Four — your eyes slowly open, you feel clarity and freshness... Five — full awakening! Stretch, smile, take a deep breath."

Post-Trance Discussion

After emerging, ask the subject what they felt. Typically, people describe pleasant relaxation, eyelid heaviness, "falling" into darkness. Never say: "You were in a trance" — say: "You entered the state excellently, your subconscious is very responsive." This strengthens the subject's belief in their own suggestibility and makes subsequent sessions easier.

Notes and Nuances

The "Breathe and Sleep" technique does not require preliminary rapport — you establish it with the command itself. If the subject didn't go to sleep on the first try, repeat the sequence 2–3 times, each time adding: "Even deeper now... even faster..." Over time, the reaction becomes automatic. Never show uncertainty — if the command didn't work, simply say: "Good, I expected that. Let's try again, only this time take a deeper breath and fully trust the process."

Working with Difficult Subjects

If the subject resists, don't enter a struggle. Use a paradoxical command: "Okay, don't fall asleep. Just take a deep breath and hold it. Now exhale. Now try to consciously keep your eyes open when I say 'sleep'... But your subconscious already knows what to do." This technique removes resistance — the subject stops fighting the suggestion and involuntarily enters trance.

💡 Tip

Practice with a partner before performing. The key element is the timbre of your voice on the word "sleep." It should be lower than usual, confident, without a questioning intonation. Say this word as if you're turning off the light in a room: one click — and darkness. Try saying "sleep" in front of a mirror 10–15 times until you feel the intonation becomes both natural and commanding. The true Kayfabe approach requires complete faith in your suggestion — if you don't believe, the subject won't believe either.

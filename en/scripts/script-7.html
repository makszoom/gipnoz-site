Shock Induction "The Jerk"

⚡ Instant inductions

📋 When to Use

When you have only 1–2 seconds to put a subject into a trance. Ideal for stage, live performances, and demonstrating the "miracle" of hypnosis before an audience. The technique uses the body's natural reflex to a sudden shift in balance — consciousness "reboots" for an instant, and in that window you deliver the suggestion. Requires some physical coordination from the hypnotist, but the result is worth it. Suitable for subjects of any suggestibility level if performed technically correctly.

📝 Script

Preparation and Position

The subject stands facing you, feet roughly shoulder-width apart, arms hanging freely. You stand slightly to the side — right foot slightly forward, left foot back for stability. Explain to the subject: "I'm going to touch your head and ask you to just stand there and do nothing. Your body will react on its own. Just trust the process." Establish eye contact, smile. Your calmness and confidence will transfer to the subject. No tension in your voice or posture.

Step 1. Distracting Attention

Place one hand (your right, if right-handed) on the back of the subject's head, lightly touching. Warm palm, soft touch. With the other hand, take their wrist or forearm. Say calmly: "Look into my eyes. Something unusual is about to happen, but you are completely safe. Just relax..." Your voice should sound monotonous, lulling. The subject relaxes, expecting a standard induction.

Step 2. The Jerk — Creating Shock

Without warning: sharply pull the subject toward you by the wrist while simultaneously pushing their head in the opposite direction. The movement should be short and fast — not strong, but sudden. The subject loses balance, their consciousness "freezes" for a split second from the surprise. Important: don't cause pain — the amplitude of movement is small, only 15–20 centimeters. It's a jerk, not a hit. Practice on a soft object to gauge the force.

Step 3. Catch and Fixation

At the same moment when the subject loses balance and their consciousness is disoriented, catch them with one hand behind the back (under the shoulder blades), and with the other — gently but firmly cover their eyes with your palm or touch their forehead. Your body becomes support — you don't let them fall, but instead take their weight onto yourself. The subject ends up in your arms — literally. Position: one hand on the eyes/forehead, the other supporting the back. Stand steadily, feet shoulder-width apart.

Step 4. Trance Suggestion

At the moment of fixation — literally 0.5 seconds after the jerk — clearly and confidently say: "Sleep!" One word, no extra phrases. And immediately, without pausing, add: "Deep, peaceful sleep. Your whole body relaxes. You hear only my voice and completely fall into a state of peace. Every muscle lets go. Your breathing becomes steady and deep. With every moment, you go deeper and deeper..."

Step 5. Verbal Deepening

Having confirmed that the subject is not resisting and their breathing has changed (become deeper and more rhythmic), begin standard deepening. Hold them in a stable position, fixing their torso with one hand: "Imagine you're falling into a soft, warm darkness... like a feather slowly landing on water... lower and lower... with every word I speak, deeper... You hear my voice and feel completely safe... Everything is good. You are in a trance."

Step 6. Trance Depth Check

As soon as you feel the subject's body has become completely relaxed (heavy) in your hands, perform a quick check: "Now I'll lift your arm — it will stay in this position, like dead weight..." — and lift their hand by the wrist. If the arm stays suspended without effort — the subject is in a good trance. If it falls — perform additional deepening: "That's okay. You're still in trance. Now you'll go even deeper... One — deeper... Two — deeper... Three — complete peace."

Working in Trance

When the depth is sufficient, sit or lay the subject down (depending on the scenario). You can deliver suggestions for confidence, relieve stress, anchor a post-hypnotic trigger, or simply let them enjoy the state of deep rest. Shock induction often produces a very deep trance, so suggestions take particularly well. Use this window of opportunity — your words will have maximum effect now.

Exiting the Trance

"In a few moments, I will return you to your normal state. You will feel rested and full of energy. On the count of five — full awakening. One — internal processes are completing, deep calm... Two — energy begins to fill your body, you feel lightness... Three — wiggle your fingers, take a gentle breath... Four — your eyes slowly open, your gaze is clear... Five — wake up! Fully here and now. Stretch, smile, take a deep breath in and out. How do you feel?"

Post-Trance Discussion

After emerging, ask: "What did you feel at the moment of the jerk?" Typical answers: "I felt like I fell into darkness," "I had the feeling I lost consciousness for a second," "I don't remember anything after your command." Explain to the subject that this is a normal reaction — shock entry into trance bypasses the critical factor of consciousness. Strengthen their faith in the process: "Your subconscious reacts very quickly. That's a rare ability. Next time will be even easier and faster."

Safety Guidelines

Never perform shock induction if the subject has neck or spine problems, epilepsy, cardiovascular disease, or has recently had surgery. Always warn about physical contact beforehand. The jerk should be sharp but brief — without excessive force. If the subject is physically larger than you, use a different technique. Your goal is to shift their balance, not fight their weight. Practice on lighter partners before performing the technique on stage.

Stage Adaptation

On stage, "The Jerk" looks very impressive. Add a dramatic gesture: pause before the jerk, raise your free hand to get the audience's attention, and only then execute the movement. The audience sees the person "losing consciousness" from a single touch — this creates a strong impression. After entering trance, you can dramatically lay the subject on the floor or seat them on a chair, adding: "Full, deep somnambulistic trance... He hears only my voice."

Working with Resistance

Some subjects may reflexively tense up at the moment of the jerk, blocking entry into trance. In this case, don't try to hold them by force. Simply release, smile, and say: "Great reaction! Your body is protecting itself — that's normal. Let's try again, only this time I'll ask you not to think, but to simply let your body do what it does. Trust me. You are safe. One more time..." Often the second attempt works because the subject already knows what to expect and doesn't waste energy on resistance.

💡 Tip

The main secret of "The Jerk" is not force, but suddenness. Imagine you're catching a falling glass — you do it sharply and gently at the same time. Same concept: you're not yanking the person, but simply removing their point of balance for a millisecond. Practice with your eyes closed — develop muscle memory. Also: your own state must be perfectly calm. If you're nervous, the subject will feel it through your touch. Breathe deeply, be in your resource — and your confidence itself will become the hypnosis.

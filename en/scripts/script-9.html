Phenomenon Catalepsy Stage

Body Catalepsy (Bridge)

🔬 Hypnosis phenomena

📋 When to Use

Full body catalepsy — the "human plank" — is the signature move of any stage hypnotist. The phenomenon demonstrates maximum trance depth, the somnambulistic stage, when muscles become so rigid that the body can be placed between two chairs like a bridge. Use this script after the subject has already demonstrated hand or eyelid catalepsy. Don't attempt the "bridge" on a first session with an unfamiliar subject — you need to be sure they are deep enough in trance. For the stage — the perfect finale trick that produces a wow-effect on the audience.

📝 Script

Preparation and Entry into Trance

Use any basic induction — progressive relaxation, gaze fixation, or instant induction. Make sure the subject is at least at a medium trance level. Check: "I will lift your arm — it will stay in the air. You won't hold it up — it will freeze by itself." If the arm stays raised without effort — you can move to body catalepsy. If the arm falls — deepen the trance with counting technique or stair visualization.

Step 1. Body Preparation

The subject is standing, you are beside them. Speak slowly, rhythmically, with suggestive intonation: "Now I will count to three, and your whole body will become hard as stone. But you will remain in a deep, calm trance. You won't feel any discomfort — only strength and stillness. One — imagine your body is filling with liquid metal... it pours into your legs, rises higher... Two — the metal hardens, every muscle becomes solid and strong... your spine is like a steel rod... Three — your whole body is frozen. You stand like a statue. You cannot move — and you don't need to. You feel only peace and rigid stillness."

Step 2. Leg Catalepsy Check

Check the tone: "Your legs are like concrete pillars. They hold you securely and motionlessly. I'm going to try to tilt you now — don't resist, just be like a block of stone." Gently push the subject's shoulder — the body should sway as a whole, like a monolithic structure. If the legs bend or the subject loses balance — add the suggestion: "Even harder. Your legs are like reinforced concrete piles. You are immovable."

Step 3. Upper Body Catalepsy

"Your back is a steel plate. Your neck is like an oak branch. Your torso is a monolith. I'm going to place your head on my hand now — and you will stay in this position. You are like a bridge stretching into the distance. Look straight ahead. Your gaze is fixed. You are looking at one point, and nothing can distract you. Complete stillness. Complete peace within this stillness."

Step 4. Preparing the "Bridge"

Place two chairs (or benches) roughly the length of the subject's body apart. The subject stands between them. "Now I'm going to lay you on these chairs. Your body will remain hard as a board. You won't feel any discomfort — only the sensation of floating in the air, supported by your own rigidity. You are safe. I'm right here. Ready?"

Step 5. Laying on the "Bridge"

With one hand supporting the subject's neck and head, and the other under the knees, slowly lower their body so that the head is on one chair and the heels on the other. The body is taut like a string. Important: weight should be evenly distributed between the heels (not ankles) and the back of the head/upper back. Continue the suggestion: "You are a bridge. Rigid, reliable, immovable. Nothing can bend you. You are frozen in this position. Your breathing is deep and steady. You are in a full trance."

Step 6. Demonstration (you may sit or stand on the subject)

When the body is fully fixed and not sagging, you can (with the subject's permission and considering your weight and their physical condition) enhance the demonstration. Important: weight should be distributed on the pelvis, not the lower back. It's better to use light hand pressure or ask someone from the audience to press gently on the torso. Say: "Look — full catalepsy. The body is hard as stone. Consciousness is off, the subconscious controls the body directly. This is the somnambulistic depth of trance."

Step 7. Removing from the Bridge

"Now I will return you to a vertical position. On the count of three, your body will become soft and flexible. One — I lift you up... two — your body gently returns to normal... three — you are standing upright, your body relaxed, but you are still in a deep trance. So good. So calm. You feel lightness throughout your body."

Deepening After Catalepsy

After catalepsy is released, the subject often falls into an even deeper trance. Use this: "Now that your body is soft and relaxed again, you can sink even deeper... As if you're lying on a cloud that's gently lowering you down... into the deepest, most peaceful place of your subconscious. There is no time here. Only peace. I will be with you the whole time."

Working with Suggestions

Now you can conduct therapeutic work. Catalepsy itself is a powerful resource. Suggest to the subject a feeling of strength, unbreakability, confidence: "Remember this feeling of absolute rigidity and stability. Now it is always with you. When life tries to bend you — you remember this sensation and remain unbendable. You are a bridge. You are strength. You are stability."

Exiting the Trance

"Now I will bring you out of the trance. You will feel rested, full of strength and energy. On the count of five — full awakening. One — deep breath in, feel the body sensation returning... Two — energy fills every cell, you feel strength and lightness... Three — wiggle your fingers, make a small movement with your shoulders... Four — your eyes open, your gaze is clear and fresh... Five — wake up! Fully here, rested, refreshed. How do you feel?"

Post-Trance Discussion

After emerging, ask: "What do you remember? Did you feel your body?" Subjects typically describe the sensation that the body was "not theirs," "numb," "like wood," "I couldn't move even if I wanted to." Praise them: "You reached the somnambulistic stage — the deepest level of trance. Your subconscious fully took control of your body. This is a rare and valuable state. Only a few percent of people can achieve it on the first try."

Safety Guidelines for the "Bridge"

It is strictly forbidden to perform the "bridge" on people with spinal problems, hernias, osteochondrosis, pregnancy, excess weight (if you're not sure of the support strength), as well as on the elderly. Always use two sturdy chairs of the same height with soft padding (place a folded towel at the head/neck support point). Remove the subject's shoes for better heel grip on the chair surface. Never leave the subject in the "bridge" position unattended. Maximum time in this position — 1–2 minutes. At any sign of discomfort, immediately remove the subject. Safety comes first.

💡 Tip

The secret to successful body catalepsy is not in the strength of the suggestion, but in the sequence. Always achieve hand and leg catalepsy separately first, before moving to full catalepsy. This creates "muscle memory" of the state in the subject. One more nuance: when laying the subject on the chairs, make sure their lower back doesn't sag — the body should be truly straight, like a board. If you see a sag, add the suggestion: "Tighten your glutes and abdomen, imagine a steel beam pulling you taut along your spine." This image usually helps achieve the needed rigidity.
